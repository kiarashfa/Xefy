Quiche Lorraine comes from the region of Lorraine in north-eastern France <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>. Its earliest versions are dated to the sixteenth century <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-quiche"/>, and they had a bread-dough case rather than pastry <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-quiche"/>.

The name may derive from the German *Kuchen*, a term for similar dishes, but its origin is uncertain <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-quiche"/>.

The original in Lorraine is *quiche au lard*, made with cream, egg and smoked bacon and no cheese <Cite ref="cooks-quiche"/>. Cyprus Mail gives the same filling as eggs, cream and lardons <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>.

Cheese is a later addition. Cyprus Mail says cooks added it in the early twentieth century, as the dish moved into French cities <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>. CooksInfo calls it reputedly a relatively modern addition <Cite ref="cooks-quiche"/>.

Purists insist that true Quiche Lorraine should contain only eggs, cream and bacon. CooksInfo reports that Lorraine purists treat anything more as "fiction" <Cite ref="cyp-quiche"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-quiche"/>. Many families in Lorraine now put Gruyère in their quiche, and French cooking schools teach it with Gruyère <Cite ref="cooks-quiche"/>.
