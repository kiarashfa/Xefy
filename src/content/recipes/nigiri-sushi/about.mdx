Nigiri is a mound of vinegared rice with a slice of fish laid over it, pressed by hand into
an oval. Nippon.com says that the nigiri form was invented by the sushi chef Hanaya Yohei about
two hundred years ago, and that he opened a full shop in Ryōgoku in 1824, after starting out at
a yatai food stall <Cite ref="nippon-nigiri"/>. The article's evidence is a description of his
shop by the chronicler Morisada, which is not a direct claim that Yohei invented the form.

Food in Japan is more cautious. It says the first maker's identity is uncertain, that Yohei is
widely credited with popularising an early form of nigiri, and that the modern form is dated to
the Bunsei era of 1818 to 1830 <Cite ref="fij-nigiri"/>. The two accounts differ on whether
Yohei made the form or refined it.

The fish was cured because there was no refrigeration. Food in Japan says that raw fish spoiled
fast without it, and that chefs salted, marinated or cooked the catch before service, habits that
began as practical measures and also made the fish taste better <Cite ref="fij-nigiri"/>.
Nippon.com describes kohada, a fish of the herring family, being firmed in vinegar and sold from
wooden boxes in the streets <Cite ref="nippon-nigiri"/>.
