The early salade niçoise was built on tomatoes, anchovies, olives and peppers, and dressed with oil, vinegar and mustard <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-nicoise"/>. Tuna and small boiled potatoes were added over the years <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>. Whether they belong is the contested point.

The purist standard is Jacques Médecin's *Cuisine niçoise*. He wrote that one should never include boiled potato or any other boiled vegetable in the salad <Cite ref="wiki-nicoise"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>. The Cercle de la Capelina d'Or certifies Nice restaurants against his standard, and rejects green beans, potatoes, sweet corn, mayonnaise, shallots and lemon <Cite ref="wiki-nicoise"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>.

Julia Child's version in *Mastering the Art of French Cooking* (1961) includes green beans and tuna <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-nicoise"/>. CooksInfo credits her with adding the tuna and with insisting on green beans and tomatoes, and on tossing each ingredient separately rather than mixing everything together <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>. This recipe follows her version, and keeps the boiled potatoes and green beans that the purist standard rejects <Cite ref="wiki-nicoise"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-nicoise"/>.
