The word *börek* traces to a Turkish verb, *bürmek*, meaning to twist or roll,
and the earliest documented uses of the name are Anatolian: a thirteenth-century
mention by the poet Mevlana, and a Kipchak-Turkic dictionary from the early
fourteenth century that defines *börek* as dough stuffed with meat
<Cite ref="wiki-burek"/>. Where the pastry itself came from before that name is
disputed. One account traces it to *en tyritas plakountas*, a layered
Byzantine cheese dish descended from Greek and Roman baking; another credits
the Turkic tradition of *yufka*, a thin flatbread layered with butter and
filling, carried west by nomadic groups out of Central Asia
<Cite ref="wiki-burek"/>. Both theories agree that a recognisable form of the
pastry was already established by the time the Ottoman Empire began its
Balkan expansion.

Burek reached Bosnia with that expansion in the mid-fifteenth century, and
Bosnian cooks did more than adopt it: the dough was worked thinner, the shape
was tightened into the spiral coil the pastry is now most associated with
there, and meat became its defining filling <Cite ref="croatiaweek-burek"/>.
That last point hardened into a naming rule that still holds locally. In
Bosnia, the coiled filo pastry is called burek only when it is filled with
meat; the same pastry filled with cheese is *sirnica*, and with spinach is
*zeljanica*, both classed more generally as *pita* rather than burek
<Cite ref="wiki-burek"/>. The distinction is not observed uniformly across the
region — in Croatia and Serbia, "burek" is commonly used for any filling,
cheese and even fruit included <Cite ref="croatiaweek-burek"/> — but within
Bosnia itself, asking for burek and receiving anything other than meat is
treated as a genuine mix-up rather than a regional variant.
