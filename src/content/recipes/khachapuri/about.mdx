Khachapuri is a Georgian bread filled with cheese. Its three best-known forms are the Imeretian, which is the most common, a round folded around its cheese; the Adjarian, a boat-shaped bread that is finished at the table; and the Megrelian, which has cheese inside and on top <Cite ref="natgeo-khachapuri"/> <Cite ref="georgia-megrelian"/>.

The article gives Esma Kunchulia's explanation of the name, which combines the Georgian words for cheese curds and bread, khacho and puri <Cite ref="natgeo-khachapuri"/>. The word is first recorded in writing in 1725 <Cite ref="natgeo-khachapuri"/>. Greek writers of the fifth century BC are said to mention a khachapuri-like dish in Colchis, but the article describes the full history of the dish as murky <Cite ref="natgeo-khachapuri"/>.

All three versions given are raised with yeast. The older Megrelian dough was different: Georgia Travel describes it as leavened with sourdough and kneaded in milk or whey <Cite ref="georgia-megrelian"/>.

The forms are finished differently. In the Adjarian, the diner breaks a yolk into the cheese and dabs butter onto the crust <Cite ref="natgeo-khachapuri"/>. In the Megrelian, sulguni grated with egg yolk is spread over the top, and a dollop of butter goes on after baking <Cite ref="georgia-megrelian"/>.
