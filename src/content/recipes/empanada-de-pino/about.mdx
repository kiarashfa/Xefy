The empanada is older than Chile's version of it by centuries. Its distant
ancestor is usually traced to the Persian *sambusak* of the ninth and tenth
centuries, a wheat pastry wrapped around seasoned meat and prized by
merchants precisely because the crust kept the filling clean on a journey
<Cite ref="dinamo-empanada"/>. The idea travelled both east into South Asia
and west into the Iberian peninsula, where it is on record by the thirteenth
century: the *Cantigas de Santa María*, compiled under Alfonso X of Castile
around 1282, includes in its fifty-seventh song an anecdote about a thief
choking on a stolen chicken empanada
<Cite ref="wiki-empanada-pino"/><Cite ref="laregion-empanada-pino"/>.

Spanish colonists carried the pastry to Chile, where the earliest known local
reference comes from Francisco Núñez de Pineda y Bascuñán's captivity
narrative, describing a Mapuche cacique sharing empanadas among other foods
during his six months as a prisoner in the seventeenth century
<Cite ref="dinamo-empanada"/>. The historian Eugenio Pereira Salas documented
how the dish became fully embedded in Chilean life by the early nineteenth
century: a 1807 food tariff set by the Cabildo of Concepción lists empanadas
alongside bread and cooked hominy as staple, regulated foods
<Cite ref="dinamo-empanada"/>.

The filling's name is where Iberian and Mapuche threads meet. *Pino* is
usually traced to the Mapudungun word for a minced mixture of meat and
onion, adopted into Chilean Spanish as the dish took its modern shape —
though accounts of the exact word differ enough between sources that the
precise etymology should be treated as reasonably well established rather
than settled to the letter <Cite ref="dinamo-empanada"/>. What is not in
question is the result: an empanada built on far more onion than meat,
sweetened by the long, slow cooking of that onion, and finished with raisins,
a wedge of hard-boiled egg and a whole black olive — a combination distinct
enough from any other country's empanada that it functions as a mark of
Chilean identity on its own.
