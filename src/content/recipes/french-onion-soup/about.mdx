French onion soup is a soup of onions cooked in stock, with bread and cheese on top. Its invention is not known. VICE says it is nearly impossible to know how it was first invented, and that it likely predates the royal stories by centuries <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>. Taillevent's fourteenth-century *Viandier* describes onions cooked in butter and topped with pea purée or water and verjus <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>.

The two royal stories are competing accounts. One credits Louis XV, who is said to have come home from a hunt with only onions, butter and Champagne in his cupboards. The other credits Stanislas Leszczynski, the duke of Lorraine, who tasted the soup at an inn, according to Alexandre Dumas's *Grand dictionnaire de cuisine* of 1873 <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-onion"/>.

The gratinée, with bread and melted cheese, is credited to restaurants around the Les Halles market in Paris, including La Poule au Pot, Chez Baratte and Au Pied de Cochon, which added grated cheese and browned the bowls under the grill <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-onion"/>. VICE says the market's porters, the *forts des Halles*, ate the soup for breakfast <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>.

Gruyère is the cheese of the modern version. Comté and Emmental are also used <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-onion"/>. Onion soup has long been deemed a hangover cure, a tradition that may have started at the market <Cite ref="vice-onion"/>.
