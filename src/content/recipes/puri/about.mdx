Puri is a deep-fried shell of wheat dough <Cite ref="scroll-puri"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia-puri"/>. It has no leavening at all. Nothing in the dough rises before the frying; the puff comes from the steam that forms inside the round as it meets the oil. That places it at the far end of the leavening range from yeasted breads like the pita, where a rise is what gives the dough its structure.

Its origin is not settled. A story now shared widely online credits Draupadi, of the Mahabharata, with inventing the dish during the exile of the Pandavas, when she is said to have made it from leftover dough and potato curry. Rohan Banerjee, writing in Scroll, describes the story as internet folklore <Cite ref="scroll-puri"/>.

The archaeologist and culinary anthropologist Kurush Dalal, quoted in the same essay, describes the puri as "a vessel" that evolved from the kachori <Cite ref="scroll-puri"/>. On that account the puri is a descendant of another fried food rather than a dish with a single inventor.
