Cassoulet is a stew of white haricot beans cooked slowly with pork and preserved duck or goose. Three towns of Languedoc, Castelnaudary, Carcassonne and Toulouse, each claim the authentic version, and the sources describe the claims as disputed <Cite ref="wiki-cassoulet"/>, <Cite ref="natgeo-cassoulet"/>.

The ranking that gives the three towns their rivalry is credited to the chef Prosper Montagné, who called Castelnaudary "God the Father", Carcassonne "God the Son" and Toulouse the "Holy Spirit" <Cite ref="natgeo-cassoulet"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-cassoulet"/>.

The sources disagree on the contents. Joël Robuchon's *French Regional Food*, written with the historian Loïc Bienassis, gives Castelnaudary generally confit goose or duck, Toulouse mutton and the city's sausage, and Carcassonne pork chops <Cite ref="natgeo-cassoulet"/>. Carcassonne's form is the least settled of the three. The recipes here follow Castelnaudary and Toulouse.

Robuchon himself writes that the town distinctions mask many others, and National Geographic's writer judges them "hardly worth the paper they're written on", because no two sources agree <Cite ref="natgeo-cassoulet"/>. The same article says white beans cannot have reached the region before the sixteenth century, and calls the story of a siege in the Hundred Years' War "almost certainly a fiction" <Cite ref="natgeo-cassoulet"/>.
