Naan is a yeasted flatbread. Its dough is raised with yeast and enriched with yoghurt and ghee, which is what separates it from unleavened breads such as roti and puri. Pressed against the hot wall of a tandoor, it puffs and blisters within minutes. On a tray or in a skillet the same dough is cooked on a flat surface instead, so the two versions are one bread at two temperatures.

The bread is well recorded in the literature of the Indian subcontinent. Amir Khusrau, an Indo-Persian poet whose works belong to the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, wrote about two kinds of naan <Cite ref="scroll-naan"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia-naan"/>.

The two breads Khusrau describes were different in thickness and in how they were baked. The naan-e-tanuk was a thin bread, so clear that a face could be seen through it. The naan-i-tanuri was thicker, baked in a clay tandoor, and puffed up like a dome <Cite ref="scroll-naan"/>. The essay has the thicker bread rise "out of joy for being included among royal dishes" <Cite ref="scroll-naan"/>.
