A frittata is an Italian egg dish. Its name comes from the Italian for fried, from
the verb friggere, and the word was originally a general term for cooking eggs in a
frying pan <Cite ref="wiki-frittata"/> <Cite ref="dictionary-frittata"/>. In Italy the name was once used for any egg
dish cooked in butter or oil in a pan <Cite ref="dictionary-frittata"/>, which is
why it describes a method more than a recipe.

The English word is recorded from 1930 to 1935, and it came into English from the
Italian for omelette <Cite ref="dictionary-frittata"/>. Outside Italy the frittata was
long seen as an equivalent of the omelette <Cite ref="wiki-frittata"/>
<Cite ref="dictionary-frittata"/>.

What separates the frittata from the omelette is the method. The fillings are stirred
into the raw beaten egg rather than laid over it, the eggs are beaten hard so that they
hold air, and the whole is set slowly over a low heat, without being folded over its
filling. The top is finished under a grill or in the oven.

Because it is set through rather than folded, a frittata cuts cleanly into wedges, and
it is usually served warm or at room temperature.
