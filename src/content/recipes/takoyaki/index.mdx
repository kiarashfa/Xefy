<Step id="prep-octopus">
  Cut the <Qty ref="octopus"/> into small dice, one piece for each ball. One serving is one ball,
  so the batch is counted by the piece rather than by the plate. This takes
  <Dur step="prep-octopus"/>, and it runs while the kombu steeps.
</Step>

<Step id="prep-toppings">
  Finely chop the <Qty ref="spring-onion"/> and the <Qty ref="pickled-ginger"/>, and keep them in
  separate small piles on a plate. Set out the <Qty ref="aonori"/> ready to scatter. This takes
  <Dur step="prep-toppings"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="make-batter">
  Sift the <Qty ref="flour"/> into a bowl and pour the strained dashi over it, whisking as you go.
  Beat in the <Qty ref="egg"/> and whisk until the batter is smooth and has no lumps of flour. Leave
  it to rest while the pan heats. This takes <Dur step="make-batter"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="heat-pan">
  Set the takoyaki pan over a medium flame. Wipe every hole with a little of the <Qty ref="oil"/> on
  folded kitchen paper, and leave the pan until it is hot enough that the batter sizzles on contact.
  This takes <Dur step="heat-pan"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="cook-takoyaki">
  Pour the batter into each hole until it is nearly full, then drop a piece of the
  <Qty ref="octopus"/>, some of the <Qty ref="spring-onion"/> and a little of the
  <Qty ref="pickled-ginger"/> into each one. Once the base has set, turn each ball with a pick a
  quarter turn at a time, drawing the loose batter from the edges into the gaps, until the balls are
  round and golden all over. Refill the holes and repeat until all the batter is used. This takes
  <Dur step="cook-takoyaki"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="finish-takoyaki">
  Arrange the balls on a warm plate, brush them with the <Qty ref="sauce"/> and drizzle the
  <Qty ref="mayo"/> over them in lines. Scatter the <Qty ref="aonori"/> on top, and serve at once
  while the centres are still soft. This takes
  <Dur step="finish-takoyaki"/>.
</Step>
