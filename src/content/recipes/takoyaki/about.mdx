Takoyaki are small balls of wheat batter, cooked in a pan with round holes around pieces of
octopus and turned by hand as they set. The standard account credits Tomekichi Endo, of the
Aizuya stall in Osaka, with the octopus version, and dates it to October 1935 <Cite ref="fij-takoyaki"/>.
Food in Japan gives the shop's own version of how the octopus came in: a customer told Endo that cooks
in Akashi put octopus in their dumplings instead of meat, and Endo tried it and named the result
takoyaki in 1935 <Cite ref="fij-takoyaki"/>.

The inventor is not settled. The official tourism site for Osaka says there are various theories
about who invented takoyaki <Cite ref="osaka-info-takoyaki"/>.

Takoyaki has earlier relatives in Osaka. Choboyaki were molded batter snacks sold from the late
Meiji through the Taishō era, and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, as Food in Japan
reports it, holds that takoyaki derives from them <Cite ref="fij-takoyaki"/>. Osaka-info says that
takoyaki may have come from choboyaki and from rajioyaki, which were popular in the Taishō and Shōwa
periods, and that takoyaki replaced the water in the choboyaki batter with dashi and added octopus
<Cite ref="osaka-info-takoyaki"/>. Food in Japan records testimony that some rajioyaki already held
octopus before 1935, and says the reference books disagree on how the two dishes relate
<Cite ref="fij-takoyaki"/>.

Akashiyaki, the egg dumplings of the city of Akashi in Hyōgo, is a related dish rather than a clear
ancestor. Akashi City says Osaka vendors came to study those dumplings, which later inspired
takoyaki, and that there are several theories about the roots of the Akashi dumplings
<Cite ref="fij-takoyaki"/>.

The octopus was plentiful in Osaka Bay, and takoyaki is presented as a way of using all of it
<Cite ref="osaka-info-takoyaki"/>. The sauce and mayonnaise topping is said to owe something to
okonomiyaki after the war, and takoyaki shops began appearing across Osaka in the 1950s
<Cite ref="osaka-info-takoyaki"/>.
