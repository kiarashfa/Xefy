notes:
  techniqueRationale:
    - stepId: boil-broth
      text: Tonkotsu is made by boiling the bones hard for many hours, not by simmering them. The fat and collagen from the marrow break down and emulsify into the water, which is what turns the broth white and thick. A gentle simmer for the same time gives a thin, clear stock with none of that body.
  commonPitfalls:
    - stepId: boil-broth
      text: Topping up the pot with cold water stops the boil and the emulsion with it. Boiling water is added instead, and the pot is kept at a hard boil with the bones always covered.

<Step id="blanch-bones">
  Put the <Qty ref="bones"/> and the <Qty ref="trotters"/> into a large pot of cold water and bring
  it to the boil. Boil them hard for <Dur step="blanch-bones"/>, then drain them, rinse off the
  scum and scrub the pot clean before the bones go back in.
</Step>

<Step id="boil-broth">
  Return the bones and trotters to the clean pot with the <Qty ref="water"/> and the
  <Qty ref="ginger"/>. Bring it to a hard, rolling boil and keep it there, topping up with boiling
  water so the bones stay covered, for <Dur step="boil-broth"/>. The broth turns cloudy and then
  white as the fat and collagen come out of the bones.
</Step>

<Step id="braise-chashu">
  Put the <Qty ref="pork"/> into a pan with the <Qty ref="soy-braise"/>, the
  <Qty ref="mirin-braise"/> and the <Qty ref="sake"/>, and add enough water to cover it halfway.
  Cover and simmer gently for <Dur step="braise-chashu"/>, turning once, until the pork is
  tender. Leave it to cool in its liquid.
</Step>

<Step id="boil-eggs">
  Lower the <Qty ref="egg"/> into boiling water and cook them for <Dur step="boil-eggs"/>, then
  cool them under cold water and peel them.
</Step>

<Step id="marinate-eggs">
  Put the peeled eggs into a bag with a little of the chashu braising liquid and leave them for
  <Dur step="marinate-eggs"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="make-tare">
  Warm the <Qty ref="soy-tare"/>, the <Qty ref="mirin-tare"/> and the <Qty ref="garlic"/> together
  in a small pan for <Dur step="make-tare"/>, until the garlic is fragrant. Divide the tare
  between two bowls.
</Step>

<Step id="strain-broth">
  Strain the broth through a fine sieve into a clean pan, letting the bones drain without
  pressing, and keep the broth hot at a low simmer. Skim any surface fat that you do not want.
  This takes <Dur step="strain-broth"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="cook-noodles">
  Cook the <Qty ref="noodles"/> in a large pan of boiling water for <Dur step="cook-noodles"/>,
  until just tender, then drain them well.
</Step>

<Step id="assemble-bowls">
  Ladle the hot broth over the tare in each bowl and stir. Add the drained noodles, then the sliced
  chashu, the halved marinated eggs, the <Qty ref="menma"/>, the <Qty ref="spring-onion"/> and the
  <Qty ref="nori"/>, and serve at once.
</Step>
