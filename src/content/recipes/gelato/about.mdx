Gelato is a dessert of milk and sugar, churned as it freezes so that it stays soft
and dense. Eggs are not a fixed part of it. Artisanal gelato often leaves them out,
while ice cream is made with eggs as a rule, and some gelato flavours, crema among
them, do carry yolks or whites <Cite ref="dissapore-gelato"/>. The base is usually
cow's milk, though many flavours add fresh cream <Cite ref="dissapore-gelato"/>.

One of the first attested recipes, from the sixteenth century, used milk, cream, snow,
egg white, sugar, salt and lemon <Cite ref="dissapore-gelato"/>.

The origin is contested. Some sources credit the Florentine figures Cosimo Ruggieri and
Bernardo Buontalenti, and others credit Sicilian cooks who gradually modified the
sherbet <Cite ref="wiki-gelato"/> <Cite ref="dissapore-gelato"/>. Dissapore sets out the
same two lines with a third: Sicilian use of the snow of Etna to chill sweet drinks,
with no date given, the Medici court in the sixteenth century, and the Sicilian
Francesco Procopio dei Coltelli and his café in Paris in the late seventeenth century
<Cite ref="dissapore-gelato"/>.
