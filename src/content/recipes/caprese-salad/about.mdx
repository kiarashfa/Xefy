Insalata caprese takes its name from Capri, the island in the Bay of Naples where
it is believed to have originated <Cite ref="wiki-caprese"/> <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/>. The origin itself is
disputed. The Grand Hôtel Quisisana is the place most often named as its birthplace
<Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/> <Cite ref="wiki-caprese"/>, and the founder of the Futurist
movement, Filippo Tommaso Marinetti, held a congress there in 1926, which places the
salad in the same era without making him its author <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/>.

The oldest of the stories is a legend that places it in the time of the Emperor
Tiberius <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/> <Cite ref="wiki-caprese"/>. The rise to
fame is usually dated to the 1950s, when the dish became known internationally. One
account has the exiled King Farouk of Egypt served it on the island
<Cite ref="wiki-caprese"/> <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/>, and Le Grand Continent places the real popularity of the
salad with his 1952 stay at a rival hotel, the Gatto Bianco <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/>.

The green of the basil, the white of the mozzarella and the red of the tomato match
the colours of the Italian flag <Cite ref="wiki-caprese"/> <Cite ref="legrand-caprese"/>.
