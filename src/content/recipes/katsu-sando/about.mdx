Katsu sando is a tonkatsu cutlet between slices of soft white bread. Food in Japan places its
origin in Ueno, Tokyo, in 1935, and credits Toki Ishizaka, the proprietress of the tonkatsu
restaurant Isen, with the idea. She set the cutlet between specially shaped slices of bread, so
that it could be eaten in a few clean bites <Cite ref="fij-katsu-sando"/>. The 1935 date comes from
a note by Isen Honten, which gives the restaurant's founding as 1930 and the sandwich's creation as
1935 <Cite ref="fij-katsu-sando"/>.

The reason given for the sandwich is the geisha of the district. Geisha who visited Isen wanted to
eat without smudging their makeup, and Food in Japan presents that as the practical problem behind
the sandwich <Cite ref="fij-katsu-sando"/>. SBS gives the origin as going back to the 1930s, and
attributes its account to Isen's own website. In that account the shop's manager had toast for
breakfast, and thought of putting a cutlet between bread for easy eating. The idea was then refined
into small pieces of crustless bread, so that geisha could snack without smudging their lipstick
<Cite ref="sbs-katsu-sando"/>.

The first version used toast, from the manager's breakfast <Cite ref="sbs-katsu-sando"/>. Today the
bread is almost always shokupan, a tender pullman loaf, rather than a generic sandwich loaf
<Cite ref="jocsk-katsu-sando"/>. Food in Japan says the sandwich spread quickly across Tokyo and then
across the country <Cite ref="fij-katsu-sando"/>.
