Parmigiana di melanzane is claimed by three regions: Campania, Sicily and Emilia-Romagna
<Cite ref="wiki-parmigiana"/> <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>. Balarm gives Sicily as the
most likely origin, and says the other two regions contest it <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>.

The name has several explanations. Some accounts tie the name to Parma and its
Parmesan <Cite ref="wiki-parmigiana"/> <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>, a link that
Balarm rejects. Balarm gives the main explanation as
the Sicilian parmiciana, a wooden slatted shutter, since the fried slices were laid in
stepped rows that looked like one <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>. Wikipedia gives the
same louver explanation, with the angled slats of the shutter standing for the layers of
aubergine <Cite ref="wiki-parmigiana"/> <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>.

The Sicilian dish was built on aubergine that was fried in plenty of oil and topped with
fresh caciocavallo cheese, and the tomato sauce came later. Balarm places the tomato's
arrival at the end of the nineteenth century <Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>.

Balarm also recalls the historian Gaetano Basile remembering a Palermo tavern in which
the name of the dish was called out in the local dialect
<Cite ref="balarm-parmigiana"/>.
