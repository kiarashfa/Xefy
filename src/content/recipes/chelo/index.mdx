<Step id="rinse">
  Rinse the <Qty ref="rice"/> in several changes of cold water, swirling and draining
  each time, for <Dur step="rinse"/>, until the water runs clear of surface starch.
</Step>

<Step id="soak">
  Cover the rinsed rice with the <Qty ref="boil-water"/> and the <Qty ref="boil-salt"/>
  and leave it to soak for <Dur step="soak"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="bloom-saffron">
  Grind the <Qty ref="saffron"/> and stir it into the <Qty ref="saffron-water"/>, hot but
  not boiling, and set it aside to steep for <Dur step="bloom-saffron"/> while the rice
  soaks.
</Step>

<Step id="parboil">
  Bring the rice in its soaking water to the boil and cook for <Dur step="parboil"/>,
  until the grains have softened at the edge but are still firm at the core. Drain
  immediately in a fine sieve.
</Step>

<Step id="assemble">
  Melt half of the <Qty ref="ghee"/> in the base of a heavy, non-stick pot. Arrange the
  <Qty ref="potato"/> over the base in a single, overlapping layer. Mound the drained
  rice over the potato in a low pyramid, taking care to keep the layer of potato
  undisturbed underneath. Drizzle over the remaining ghee and the bloomed saffron
  water, then use the handle of a spoon to poke a few holes down through the mound
  to let steam escape. Spend <Dur step="assemble"/> building the layers evenly.
</Step>

<Step id="steam">
  Wrap the pot lid in a clean cloth, secure it on tightly, and set the pot over a low
  heat for <Dur step="steam"/>, until the potato at the base has set into a firm,
  golden crust and the rice on top is tender and separate.
</Step>

<Step id="unmould">
  Take the pot off the heat and let it stand briefly, then invert it over a serving
  platter in one confident motion so the crust of potato lifts free on top. This
  takes <Dur step="unmould"/>.
</Step>
