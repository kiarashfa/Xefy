Yakisoba is a stir-fry of wheat noodles, and its best-known form is the sauce yakisoba
sold from Tokyo griddle stalls. The noodles themselves are Chinese in origin. Just One Cookbook
says the dish evolved from chow mein brought to Japan by Chinese immigrants in the Taisho era,
1912 to 1926, and says that it got its start in Tokyo's Asakusa area
<Cite ref="jtc-yakisoba"/>.

Food in Japan says Chinese-style wheat noodles reached Japanese port cities in the late
nineteenth century, and that stir-fried dishes modelled on chow mein followed soon after
<Cite ref="fij-yakisoba"/>. The sauce form came from the griddle stalls. Food in Japan reports
that Tokyo stalls sold playful imitations of Western dishes seasoned with Worcestershire
sauce, and that the sauce maker Otafuku traces sauce yakisoba to these stalls in the Taisho
era. It adds that early versions apparently held only cabbage, and that the exact year remains
unclear <Cite ref="fij-yakisoba"/>.

The sauce is the part that defines the dish in Japan. It is a thick, sweet and sour fruit sauce
of the same family as the sauce for tonkatsu, and the noodles are coated in it at the end of
the stir-fry rather than cooked in it.
