What separates a "New York" cheesecake from cheesecake generally is a single ingredient switch, and it happened later than the dessert's reputation
suggests. Cream cheese itself was invented in 1872, by William Lawrence in Chester, New York, but it took until stabilised, shelf-stable versions
reached grocers in the late 1920s for bakers to build a cake around it rather than the curd cheeses American cheesecakes had used up to that point
<Cite ref="toriavey-nycheesecake"/> <Cite ref="worldchefs-nycheesecake"/>.

The credit for making that switch is disputed between two Manhattan restaurateurs who did not particularly like each other, and the two independent
accounts of the dispute do not entirely agree. Arnold Reuben said that after tasting a cheese pie at a dinner party in 1929, he got hold of the recipe
and reworked it, substituting cream cheese for the curds, and served the result at his own restaurants through the 1930s — a story both accounts
repeat <Cite ref="toriavey-nycheesecake"/> <Cite ref="worldchefs-nycheesecake"/>. Lindy's, the Broadway delicatessen opened by Leo Lindemann in 1921
and later fictionalised as "Mindy's" in Damon Runyon's stories and in *Guys and Dolls*, became at least as associated with the dessert, but where the
recipe came from is where the two accounts split: one holds that Lindemann had hired away Reuben's own baker, while the other traces Lindy's
cheesecake instead to food journalist Clementine Paddleford and chef Paul Landry, as reported by the *Los Angeles Times* in 1948
<Cite ref="toriavey-nycheesecake"/> <Cite ref="worldchefs-nycheesecake"/>. Neither version can be verified independently of the restaurant it favours,
which is the usual condition of a rivalry conducted in gossip columns rather than patent filings — but a contested recipe origin is closer to the
truth here than a tidy one would be.
