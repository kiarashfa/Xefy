Künefe belongs to the same wide family of syrup-soaked cheese pastries found
across the Levant, and the version eaten in Turkey is specifically Hatay's:
it is generally described as having developed in the province around Antakya,
just across the border from Syria, where the broader kanafeh tradition is
strongest <Cite ref="wiki-kunefe"/>. What distinguishes it from its Arab
relatives is less the syrup, which is much the same sugar-and-lemon mixture
used throughout the family, than the pairing of shredded kadayıf with a
specific local cheese rather than a semolina crust or a Palestinian brined
cheese.

That specificity is now written into law rather than left to custom. Hatay's
künefe received geographical-indication protection from Turkey's own patent
office, and in March 2023 the European Commission followed with a Protected
Geographical Indication for "Antakya Künefesi", the ninth Turkish product to
receive the designation <Cite ref="wiki-kunefe"/><Cite ref="dailysabah-kunefe"/>.
The registered specification names four ingredients — künefelik kadayıf, fresh
Antakya cheese made specifically for künefe, butter and syrup — and ties the
recipe to production methods passed down through master-and-apprentice
relationships within Hatay province, rather than to any single documented
inventor or date <Cite ref="dailysabah-kunefe"/>. That absence of a founding
story is itself informative: unlike baklava's contested Ottoman-versus-Greek
origin, künefe's identity rests on a living, protected regional practice
rather than a disputed history.
