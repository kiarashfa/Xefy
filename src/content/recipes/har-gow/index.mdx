<Step id="make-dough">
  Combine the <Qty ref="wheat-starch"/> and <Qty ref="dough-tapioca"/> in a heatproof
  bowl. Pour in the <Qty ref="boiling-water"/> in a steady stream while stirring hard,
  until the mixture turns from cloudy and loose to translucent and clumped together.
  Once cool enough to handle, knead in the <Qty ref="dough-oil"/> and
  <Qty ref="dough-salt"/> for <Dur step="make-dough"/>, until smooth and pliable.
</Step>

<Step id="rest-dough">
  Cover the dough tightly with a damp cloth or clingfilm — it dries and cracks fast —
  and rest it for <Dur step="rest-dough"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="make-filling">
  Meanwhile, combine the <Qty ref="shrimp"/>, <Qty ref="bamboo-shoots"/>,
  <Qty ref="spring-onion"/>, <Qty ref="ginger"/>, <Qty ref="sesame-oil"/>,
  <Qty ref="sugar"/>, <Qty ref="pepper"/>, <Qty ref="filling-salt"/> and
  <Qty ref="filling-tapioca"/> in a bowl and stir until the mixture turns sticky and
  holds together.
</Step>

<Step id="roll-and-fill">
  Pinch off small, even pieces of dough, keeping the rest covered. Press each piece
  flat under the broad side of a wide knife blade into a thin, near-transparent round
  about <Len cm={7}/> across. Spoon filling into the centre and pleat one edge into a
  tight fan against the other, sealing the dumpling into a crescent that stands on its
  flat base.
</Step>

<Step id="steam">
  Line a steamer basket so the dumplings cannot stick to the metal or bamboo directly,
  and set them in with space between each. Steam over rapidly boiling water for
  <Dur step="steam"/>, until the wrapper has turned fully translucent and the shrimp
  inside is opaque and pink.
</Step>
