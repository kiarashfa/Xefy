Egg dishes cooked in a vessel are far older than the omelette. The Lyon municipal library's reference service separates the two, and notes that the modern omelette is more recent than the egg preparations of antiquity <Cite ref="guichet-omelette"/>.

The word itself is documented in French from the fifteenth century. The accounts of the Tours town hall record an *amelette* on 19 February 1480, and *omelette* appears in print in 1561 in a book of cures for dogs <Cite ref="guichet-omelette"/>. Its origin is debated. The reference service reports that the sources do not settle it, and that the most common view is an alteration of earlier forms, with the flat shape of the dish compared to a blade <Cite ref="guichet-omelette"/>.

The Fondation Napoléon credits the gastronome Brillat-Savarin, whose best-known work appeared in 1825, with an omelette recipe that tells the cook to spread the mixture out on the dish <Cite ref="napoleon-omelette"/>. The rolled, pale and barely coloured omelette described here is the French form the recipe follows.
