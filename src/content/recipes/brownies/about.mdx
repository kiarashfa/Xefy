The most polished account of the brownie's invention names a place, a date and a patron. Bertha Palmer, whose husband had given her Chicago's Palmer
House Hotel, is said to have asked its pastry chef for a small, packable dessert for the boxed lunches served to women visiting the 1893 World's
Columbian Exposition. The result was a dense, fudgy square finished with walnuts and an apricot glaze <Cite ref="tastingtable-brownies"/>. The chef
who is supposed to have made it is not named in any surviving account, which is exactly the kind of gap that separates a documented recipe from a
good story about one.

A second, more folksy version places the invention in Bangor, Maine, where a housewife baking a chocolate cake is said to have forgotten the baking
powder; the batter never rose, she cut it into squares anyway, and a dessert was born by accident <Cite ref="tastingtable-brownies"/>. The story
attaches itself to a real, dated document — a "Bangor Brownies" recipe printed in the *Boston Globe* in 1905 — but the connection between the recipe's
name and the accident it is supposed to explain is not established; nothing in the record confirms a specific Bangor housewife as the dessert's
inventor <Cite ref="tastingtable-brownies"/>. What can be dated is the print trail either side of it: a molasses cake recipe appeared in the *Boston
Cooking School Cookbook* in 1896, and by 1905 Fannie Farmer had adapted her own chocolate cookie dough into a sheet-pan format close to a modern
brownie <Cite ref="tastingtable-brownies"/>.

What the competing stories agree on, more than any inventor, is that the brownie took shape in the United States in the closing years of the
nineteenth century as chocolate confections and cake batters were pushed toward a single dense, cuttable square — a form with no close equivalent in
the British or European baking that most other American desserts of the period still borrowed from <Cite ref="ushistoryscene-brownies"/>. Even the
name is unsettled: it may simply describe the colour, or it may owe something to a set of mischievous "Brownie" characters popular in children's
books of the period <Cite ref="tastingtable-brownies"/>.
