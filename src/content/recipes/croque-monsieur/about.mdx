The croque monsieur is ham and Gruyère between slices of pain de mie, with grated cheese on top, lightly salted and peppered <Cite ref="wiki-croque"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>. It is fried in clarified butter <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>.

Béchamel is not part of the classic recipe. Wikipedia says only that some brasseries add it, and CooksInfo calls the claim that a true croque monsieur requires it plain factually incorrect <Cite ref="wiki-croque"/>, <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>.

Popular wisdom holds that the sandwich was first served in 1910 at Le Trou dans le Mur, a café on the Boulevard des Capucines in Paris. CooksInfo cites the 1988 English edition of *Larousse Gastronomique* for this account <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>. The earliest recipe CooksInfo cites is in *The Belgian Cookbook* of 1915, which makes it from buttered bread with cheese and ham, fried in butter <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>.

The croque madame is the same sandwich with a fried egg on top. CooksInfo says the record does not show when or how the egg was added <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>. Some versions replace the ham with chicken, as Craig Claiborne and Julia Child both described in the 1970s <Cite ref="cooks-croque"/>.
