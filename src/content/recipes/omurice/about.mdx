Omurice is a Japanese-coined name. The word joins *omelet* and *rice*, and the dish is
usually described as a yōshoku, a Western-influenced dish made in Japan
<Cite ref="food-in-japan-omurice"/>.

Its origin is claimed by two restaurants. Food in Japan reports that Rengatei, a Ginza
restaurant founded in 1895, traces its rice omelette to about 1900, when cooks made it as
a staff meal with the rice and egg cooked together rather than wrapped. The article dates
Rengatei's menu version to about a year later, and notes that published accounts differ
on the exact dates <Cite ref="food-in-japan-omurice"/>.

The rival account is from Osaka. Just One Cookbook traces the wrapped dish to a 1925 diner
in Osaka called Hokkyokusei <Cite ref="jtc-omurice"/>. Food in Japan gives the restaurant's
own version: a regular customer with a sensitive stomach asked for plain rice with an
omelette, and the founder wrapped ketchup rice in a thin omelette for him instead
<Cite ref="food-in-japan-omurice"/>.

Both origin stories come from the restaurants themselves, and which came first is not settled.
