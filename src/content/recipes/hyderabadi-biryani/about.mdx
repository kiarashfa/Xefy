Hyderabad passed under Mughal control in the 1630s and was afterward governed by
its own line of Nizams, whose kitchens fused Mughlai cooking with local Deccani and
Telugu traditions over the following centuries <Cite ref="deccan-chronicle-biryani"/>.
Biryani itself predates that court: the wider technique of cooking rice and meat
together traces to Persian pilaf, carried into South Asia along Mughal and Arab
trade and military routes well before it took on a distinctly Hyderabadi form
<Cite ref="wiki-hyderabadi-biryani"/>.

A popular story credits the dish's invention to a chef of the first Nizam,
Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I, improvising with local spices during a mid-eighteenth-century
hunting expedition. That account is repeated widely but is folklore rather than a
documented event, and it sits alongside the more general, better-evidenced view
that Hyderabadi biryani is one regional outcome of a much older Persian pilaf
tradition rather than a single act of invention <Cite ref="wiki-hyderabadi-biryani"/>.
What is better attested is that the dish took its present form inside the Nizams'
own kitchens, where recipes were closely guarded and rarely written down, over
roughly the four centuries of Asaf Jahi rule <Cite ref="deccan-chronicle-biryani"/>.

The kacchi and pakki methods are usually described as the Hyderabadi court's own
refinement against a wider Mughal preparation: kacchi, in which raw marinated meat
is sealed under parboiled rice and cooks entirely in that shared steam, is named as
the Nizam's own signature technique, while pakki, built on meat cooked before it
ever meets the rice, is associated instead with Mughal army kitchens that needed a
faster, more forgiving version to feed large numbers of soldiers
<Cite ref="deccan-chronicle-biryani"/>. The distinction between a pulao and a
biryani more broadly is itself not a sharp one in the historical record, and both
are best understood as branches of the same one-pot tradition of rice cooked with
meat rather than as separate inventions <Cite ref="wiki-hyderabadi-biryani"/>.
