<Step id="mix-dough">
  Combine the <Qty ref="flour-dough"/>, <Qty ref="dough-water"/> and
  <Qty ref="dough-salt"/> in a bowl and bring together into a firm dough, then turn it
  out and knead for <Dur step="mix-dough"/> until smooth and no longer tacky.
</Step>

<Step id="rest-dough">
  Cover the dough and rest it for <Dur step="rest-dough"/> at room temperature, until it
  has relaxed and no longer springs back when pressed.
</Step>

<Step id="make-filling">
  Meanwhile, combine the <Qty ref="beef"/>, <Qty ref="pork"/>, <Qty ref="onion"/>,
  <Qty ref="garlic"/>, <Qty ref="coriander"/>, <Qty ref="black-pepper"/> and
  <Qty ref="filling-salt"/> in a bowl. Work in the <Qty ref="filling-water"/> a little
  at a time, mixing until the filling turns loose and wet rather than staying stiff.
</Step>

<Step id="shape">
  Roll the rested dough out on a surface dusted with the <Qty ref="flour-bench"/> and
  cut into rounds about <Len cm={10}/> across. Spoon filling into the centre of each
  round, then gather the edge up around it in narrow pleats, twisting the pleats
  together at the top into a knotted handle and pinching the join fully shut. Set the
  shaped khinkali on a floured tray, knot up, as you go.
</Step>

<Step id="boil">
  Drop the khinkali into a large pot of well-salted, gently boiling water, stirring
  once so they do not settle on the base, and cook for <Dur step="boil"/>, until they
  float and the dough is cooked through. Lift out with a slotted spoon and serve at
  once, holding each by its knotted top to eat and setting the knot itself aside
  uneaten.
</Step>
