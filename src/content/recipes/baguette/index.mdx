<Step id="mix">
  The figures on this page are per loaf. Put the <Qty ref="flour-dough"/> into a large
  bowl, add the <Qty ref="water"/>, and mix with a spatula or your hand until no dry
  flour remains. Cover the bowl.
</Step>

<Step id="autolyse">
  Leave the flour and water to stand for <Dur step="autolyse"/>, until the dough is
  shaggy, sticky and holds together.
</Step>

<Step id="knead">
  Scatter the <Qty ref="yeast"/> and the <Qty ref="salt"/> over the dough and knead it
  for <Dur step="knead"/>, until it is smooth and stretches thin without tearing.
</Step>

<Step id="first-rise">
  Put the dough back in the bowl, cover it, and leave it to rise for
  <Dur step="first-rise"/>. Stretch and fold it over on itself every half hour during
  that time, until it has grown by about half and feels airy.
</Step>

<Step id="shape">
  Tip the dough onto a bench dusted with the <Qty ref="flour-bench"/>. Divide it into
  equal pieces and shape each into a loose round, then leave them to relax for a few
  minutes. Roll each piece into a long cylinder, pinching the seam closed as you go,
  and taper the ends.
</Step>

<Step id="second-rise">
  Set the loaves seam side up on a floured cloth, folding the cloth between them so
  they hold their shape. Leave them to prove for <Dur step="second-rise"/>, until a
  fingertip pressed in leaves a dent that springs back slowly.
</Step>

<Step id="heat-oven">
  Heat the oven to <Temp c={250}/> with a baking stone or tray inside it and a second
  tray in the bottom to hold boiling water for steam. Let it heat for
  <Dur step="heat-oven"/> so the stone is fully hot before the loaves go in.
</Step>

<Step id="bake">
  Turn the loaves onto a sheet of baking paper, slash each one a few times along its
  length, and slide them onto the hot stone. Pour the boiling water into the tray below
  and shut the door at once. Bake for <Dur step="bake"/>, until deep gold and hollow
  when tapped on the base.
</Step>

<Step id="cool">
  Lift the loaves onto a rack and leave them for <Dur step="cool"/> before cutting, so
  the crumb can set.
</Step>
