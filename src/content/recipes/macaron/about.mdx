The macaron has no single birthplace, and the sources do not agree on one. The story that a chef of Catherine de Médicis brought it to France is given by Sortiraparis as "generally believed", without a named source <Cite ref="sortiraparis-macaron"/>.

The Parisian macaron, two shells sandwiched around a filling of ganache, buttercream or jam, is the form now sold in the city's shops. Sortiraparis places its appearance in the nineteenth century, under the name Macaron Gerbet, and credits Ladurée, Dalloyau and Lenôtre with popularising it <Cite ref="sortiraparis-macaron"/>. The sources do not agree on when the filled form began, and neither settles who first made it <Cite ref="sortiraparis-macaron"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-macaron"/>.

What the Parisian style fixes is the structure rather than the flavour: a smooth, domed shell with a frilled foot, made from ground almond, icing sugar and whipped egg white, and then filled. There are two ways to whip the meringue that holds the shell together, the French and the Italian, and they produce the same shell by different routes.
