Focaccia genovese is a yeasted flatbread of wheat, soft and generously oiled. The dough is pressed deep with the fingers so that the oil collects in the dimples, and it is finished with coarse salt <Cite ref="visitgenoa-focaccia"/>. The city's tourism office describes it as having very ancient origins, probably dating to the period of the Republic of Genoa, when bread was the main food of the population <Cite ref="visitgenoa-focaccia"/>.

The documented history is more exact, and more cautious. A Genoese property inventory of 1392 lists a large peel "pro fugacis", which the Gazzetta del Gusto article reads as a peel for baking directly on the oven floor. The article says it is not certain that this item is the same focaccia <Cite ref="gazzetta-focaccia"/>. Later sources are firmer. Early Genoese dictionaries and cookbooks of the nineteenth century describe a recipe close to the modern one, though the older recipes advised only a light pinch on the surface, where bakers now press the fingers in deeply <Cite ref="gazzetta-focaccia"/>.

The focaccia itself is protected. A collective trademark, set up in 1996, covers it and is held by local bakers <Cite ref="gazzetta-focaccia"/>. The same article judges that older bakers raised their focaccia with a natural starter rather than brewer's yeast, so the modern dough here, which uses dried yeast, is a later practice <Cite ref="gazzetta-focaccia"/>.
