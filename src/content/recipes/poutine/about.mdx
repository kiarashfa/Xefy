Poutine's origin is claimed by at least three small restaurants in Quebec's
Centre-du-Québec region, all within a few years of one another, and none of
them can be fully proven. At Le Lutin qui rit in Warwick, the owner Fernand
Lachance is said to have added cheese curds to a regular customer's fries in
1957 and remarked that it would make "*une maudite poutine*", a damned mess.
Gravy was added a few years later to keep the dish warm
<Cite ref="wiki-poutine"/><Cite ref="dailyhive-poutine"/>. In Drummondville,
Jean-Paul Roy of Le Roy Jucep says he began combining fries, curds and gravy
around the same period and put it on his menu under the name by the
mid-1960s, and the restaurant holds an official trademark registration for
the word "poutine" <Cite ref="wiki-poutine"/><Cite ref="dailyhive-poutine"/>.
A third establishment, La Petite Vache in Princeville, has its own version of
the story. As one account puts it, nobody will ever know for certain who
combined the three ingredients first <Cite ref="dailyhive-poutine"/>.

What is not disputed is how poorly regarded the dish was for decades after
it left its rural roots. Poutine carried a real stigma once it reached
wider Quebec society: mocked and ridiculed as unsophisticated backwoods
food or a late-night, post-drinking snack, it was something serious
Montreal kitchens would cook for their own staff but keep off the menu
<Cite ref="vice-poutine"/>. That changed only when chefs began treating it
as a dish worth refining rather than apologising for. Martin Picard opened
Au Pied de Cochon in November 2001 and became known for dishes that mixed
luxury ingredients with humble Quebec staples, chief among them a foie gras
poutine that is generally credited with changing how fine-dining audiences
saw the dish, after which upscale versions spread into restaurants across
Canada <Cite ref="tce-picard"/><Cite ref="vice-poutine"/>.

The version worth cooking at home owes nothing to that later elevation. It
is the plain original: hot fries, fresh curds, and gravy poured over both
while everything is still hot enough to matter.
