Kare raisu is classed in Japan as yōshoku, Western-style food, and the classification
describes how the dish was made as much as where it came from. Its brown colour comes
from a European-style roux, cooked from fat and flour, rather than from the spice paste
of a South Asian curry <Cite ref="scroll-curry"/>.

The first Japanese curry recipe on record appeared in 1872, in Kanagaki Robun's
*Connoisseur of Western-Style Cuisine* (*Seiyō ryōri tsū*) <Cite ref="uva-curry"/>. The
route into the country ran through the navies. British ships began calling at Japanese
harbours after 1853, and Japanese sailors learned of the curry that British sailors
ate <Cite ref="uva-curry"/>.

The Japanese navy had its own problem. A diet of rice, miso soup and pickles left its
sailors short of vitamin B1, and beriberi became more common <Cite ref="uva-curry"/>.
The navy's answer was a curry made with meat and flour, which supplied the missing
thiamine <Cite ref="scroll-curry"/>. The sailors ate it with rice, where the British
had used bread <Cite ref="uva-curry"/>.

The roux is what sets the dish apart from the curries it is often compared with. It is
a cooked fat and flour base that thickens the stew and deepens its colour
<Cite ref="scroll-curry"/>.
