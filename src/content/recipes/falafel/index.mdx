  techniqueRationale:
    - stepId: soak
      text: The chickpeas are never cooked before grinding. A raw, soaked chickpea grinds into a paste that holds together and fries firm; a cooked one turns to mush and falls apart in the oil.
  commonPitfalls:
    - stepId: grind

<Step id="soak">
  Cover the <Qty ref="chickpea"/> with the <Qty ref="soak-water"/> and leave to soak for
  <Dur step="soak"/>. The chickpeas should roughly double in size and still be raw and
  firm, not softened.
</Step>

<Step id="grind">
  Drain the chickpeas and pulse them in a food processor with the <Qty ref="onion"/>,
  <Qty ref="garlic"/>, <Qty ref="parsley"/>, <Qty ref="coriander"/>, <Qty ref="cumin"/>,
  <Qty ref="pepper"/> and <Qty ref="salt"/> for <Dur step="grind"/>, stopping while the
  mixture is still coarse and slightly grainy rather than smooth.
</Step>

<Step id="rest">
  Stir in the <Qty ref="baking-soda"/> and rest the mixture for <Dur step="rest"/> before
  shaping.
</Step>

<Step id="fry">
  Heat the <Qty ref="frying-oil"/> in a deep pan to a steady, gentle bubble. Shape the
  mixture into small patties and lower them in without crowding the pan. Fry for
  <Dur step="fry"/>, turning once, until deeply browned and firm to the touch. Lift out
  and drain.
</Step>
