Pesto alla genovese is a sauce of basil, garlic, coarse salt, pine nuts, a grated
hard cheese and olive oil, traditionally pounded together in a marble mortar with a
wooden pestle <Cite ref="wiki-pesto"/> <Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>.

The sauce is older than its basil. Genoa's garlic-based sauces came first, and
before basil other herbs, marjoram among them, were pounded in the same way
<Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>. The sauce was codified in the second half of the
nineteenth century <Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>, and the early printed recipes are
Emanuele Rossi's *La Vera Cucineria Genovese* of 1852 and Giovanni Battista Ratto's
*La Cuciniera Genovese* of 1863 <Cite ref="wiki-pesto"/> <Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>.

Ratto's *battuto alla genovese* includes pine nuts, which are absent from the
recipe in Artusi's cookbook <Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>.

Basil is sensitive to heat, and the blades of a blender can warm and oxidise the
mixture, which is the case the Gazzetta del Gusto makes for keeping to the mortar
<Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>.

The Associazione Palatifini organises a pesto championship in Genoa every two years
<Cite ref="gazzetta-pesto"/>.
