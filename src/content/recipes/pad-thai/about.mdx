Pad thai is young, and unusually well documented for it. Half its name is Chinese:
*kuai tiao* is a Teochew word for rice noodles, and the full form, *kuai tiao phat
thai*, means those noodles fried Thai-style — a construction that only makes sense if
the dish was already understood as a Thai treatment of something foreign
<Cite ref="wiki-padthai"/>. The technique is Chinese stir-frying, carried to Siam by
southern Chinese migrants; the tamarind, palm sugar, fish sauce and chilli are what
was done to it locally <Cite ref="greeley-2009"/>.

What turned it into a national dish was policy. Field Marshal Plaek Phibunsongkhram,
prime minister from 1938, issued twelve cultural mandates: the first, on 24 June 1939,
renamed Siam as Thailand, and the fifth, that November, instructed citizens to eat food
made from Thai produce <Cite ref="wiki-mandates"/>. Flooding wrecked the rice crop of
the Chao Phraya delta in 1942, and the government answered by promoting noodles —
distributing carts and recipes through the Public Welfare Department under the slogan
that noodles were your lunch <Cite ref="priceonomics-2015"/><Cite ref="wiki-padthai"/>.
A study of the period reads the whole exercise as the deliberate Thai-ization of
Chinese *kway teow* under that government <Cite ref="yao-2024"/>.

Whether the state also invented the dish is a separate question, and the popular answer
overstates the record. The story of a national recipe competition that produced pad
thai circulates widely; Phibun's own son, interviewed by *Gastronomica*, remembered it
being cooked in the family home before it became policy and could not say who devised
it <Cite ref="greeley-2009"/><Cite ref="priceonomics-2015"/>. The promotion is
documented in detail. The invention is not.

What is not in doubt is the shape of the dish: one sauce carrying sour, sweet and salt
together, noodles fried hard enough to catch on the pan, and the last adjustments left
to the person eating it — lime, chilli and peanuts arrive on the side rather than in the
wok <Cite ref="wiki-padthai"/>.
