Oyakodon's name means parent-and-child bowl. The chicken is the parent and the egg is the
child, and the dish takes its name from that pairing <Cite ref="nippon-oyakodon"/>.

Food in Japan places the dish's shaping in Meiji-era Tokyo and says it has been loved for
more than a hundred years, without giving a year <Cite ref="food-in-japan-oyakodon"/>.

The same article says the Tokyo restaurant Tamahide is widely credited with popularising
the dish <Cite ref="food-in-japan-oyakodon"/>.

The loose broth of the bowl is also the reason for a relative. Food in Japan says the
broth spilled during transport, which led to soboro don, a drier bowl of minced chicken
made as a portable version <Cite ref="food-in-japan-oyakodon"/>.
