Cobbler exists in the form it does because of a piece of colonial kitchen equipment cooks did not have. A proper pie needs an oven and a crust rolled
thin enough to hold its shape top and bottom, neither of which an open hearth or a Dutch oven over coals made easy. Settlers baking over fire instead
covered stewed or raw fruit with a dough dropped or rolled on top — closer to a savoury pudding topping than to pastry — and cooked the whole pot
directly in the embers <Cite ref="tastingtable-cobbler"/>. That workaround, not a recipe anyone set out to invent, is the origin of the dish's
characteristic dropped or cobbled crust.

The fruit came from further away than the dish's Southern reputation implies. Peaches are not native to North America; Spanish expeditions, including
Hernando de Soto's, brought them to the continent in 1539, after some eight thousand years of cultivation in China beforehand <Cite ref="museumnewsouth-cobbler"/>.
They spread through the eastern South substantially by way of Indigenous peoples' own cultivation rather than through English settlement alone, and the
orchards most associated today with the fruit — Georgia's among them — came later still <Cite ref="museumnewsouth-cobbler"/>.

Cobbler stayed a home dish, made from whatever fruit was on hand, for a long time before it reached print in a form specific to peaches: Lettice
Bryan's *The Kentucky Housewife*, published in 1839, carries what is usually cited as the first printed peach cobbler recipe, decades after cooks in
the region had plainly been making some version of it <Cite ref="museumnewsouth-cobbler"/>. It also began as something closer to a staple than a treat.
Cobbler was as likely to appear at breakfast or dinner as at the end of a meal, and it was not firmly classed as a dessert until late in the nineteenth
century — around the same period that wider peach cultivation and, later, canning turned what had been a seasonal, sometimes costly fruit into
something ordinary households could keep and cook with year-round <Cite ref="tastingtable-cobbler"/><Cite ref="museumnewsouth-cobbler"/>.
