Sukiyaki is a beef and vegetable dish that took its present form in the Meiji period, although
its name is older than its beef. Food in Japan says the word combines *suki*, a spade-like blade,
and *yaki*, to grill, and that fish and shellfish were grilled on such a blade in the Kansai
region by the middle of the Edo period. Cookbooks of 1801 and 1803 describe birds and fish cooked
this way, and beef came later <Cite ref="fij-sukiyaki"/>.

Beef spread after the port of Yokohama opened in 1859. In the Meiji period, restaurants in
Yokohama and Tokyo served *gyunabe*, a beef pot, and early versions were seasoned with miso
before soy sauce and sugar replaced it <Cite ref="fij-sukiyaki"/>. Just One Cookbook says that
the Kantō region once called its own version gyunabe, and that the name sukiyaki came to Kantō
later <Cite ref="jocsk-sukiyaki"/>.

Food in Japan, citing the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, dates the change of
name to the Great Kantō Earthquake of 1923. Most gyunabe shops in Tokyo closed after it, and the
Kansai name spread east in their place <Cite ref="fij-sukiyaki"/>. Just One Cookbook describes the
same sequence <Cite ref="jocsk-sukiyaki"/>.

The two regional styles differ in how the sauce is made. Kantō cooks use a pre-mixed warishita, a
sweet soy sauce that the beef and vegetables simmer in, and the result is even and mellow. Kansai
cooks sear the beef, then add sugar and soy directly to the pan, which gives a stronger flavour and
caramelised edges. Food in Japan calls these general tendencies rather than fixed rules, and notes
that many long-established Tokyo restaurants now sear the beef first <Cite ref="fij-sukiyaki"/>.

Each cooked piece is dipped in beaten raw egg before it is eaten. Food in Japan calls the custom
traditional but optional, and says that the reason it began is less clear <Cite ref="fij-sukiyaki"/>.
Just One Cookbook gives the folk explanation that the cold egg removes the smell of beef and keeps
the mouth from burning, and presents it as folklore <Cite ref="jocsk-sukiyaki"/>.
