Ratatouille is a Provençal stew of summer vegetables. Its name combines the Occitan *ratatolha* with the French verb *touiller*, to stir <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>.

The word is older than the dish as it is now made. Its first recorded use is 1831, in a description of a watery vegetable dish with meat <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>. National Geographic Traveller says the dish as we know it appears in writing much later, and cites Henri Heyraud's *La Cuisine à la Nice* of 1903 as one of the first French references <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>.

The confit byaldi is a restaurant version of the stew, and it is credited to Michel Guérard, who published it in his *Cuisine Minceur* in 1976 <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-confit"/>.

Thomas Keller served as food consultant on the 2007 Pixar film *Ratatouille*, and National Geographic Traveller describes him as having borrowed the dish <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-confit"/>. The same article says the dish Remy serves in the film is not, strictly speaking, a ratatouille, but a confit byaldi <Cite ref="ng-ratatouille"/>.
