<Step id="cream">
  Beat the <Qty ref="butter"/> with the <Qty ref="brown-sugar"/> and the
  <Qty ref="caster-sugar"/> for <Dur step="cream"/>, until the mixture has gone visibly
  paler and grown in volume, and no grittiness is left when a little is rubbed between
  finger and thumb.
</Step>

<Step id="beat-in-eggs">
  Beat in the <Qty ref="eggs"/> a little at a time, letting each addition disappear
  completely before the next goes in, then the <Qty ref="vanilla"/>. Take
  <Dur step="beat-in-eggs"/> over it; adding the egg too fast splits the mixture and it
  will not come back.
</Step>

<Step id="fold-dry">
  Sift the <Qty ref="flour"/> together with the <Qty ref="bicarb"/> and the
  <Qty ref="salt"/> and fold them in with a spatula over <Dur step="fold-dry"/>, stopping
  the instant no dry flour is visible. Fold the <Qty ref="chocolate-dough"/> through with
  the last few turns.
</Step>

<Step id="chill">
  Cover the bowl and leave it in the fridge for <Dur step="chill"/>. The dough should be
  firm enough to roll without sticking.
</Step>

<Step id="heat-oven">
  Meanwhile heat the oven to <Temp c={180}/> and give it <Dur step="heat-oven"/> to come
  up properly — an oven that has only just reached the dial is not the same as one that
  has settled there. Line the flattest baking trays in the house.
</Step>

<Step id="bake">
  Roll the dough into balls a little larger than a walnut and set them far apart, since
  they spread to roughly <Len cm={9}/> across. Press the <Qty ref="chocolate-top"/> onto
  the tops. Bake for <Dur step="bake"/>, until the edges have set and coloured and the
  centres are still pale and slightly domed.
</Step>

<Step id="set-on-tray">
  Leave them on the hot tray for <Dur step="set-on-tray"/> before moving them. They go on
  cooking on the metal and are too fragile to lift until the middles have set and sunk
  into the dense, ridged centre that is the whole object.
</Step>
