<Step id="slice-spring-onion">
  Slice the <Qty ref="spring-onion"/> finely and keep it in cold water so that it curls.
  Shred the <Qty ref="nori"/> with scissors. This takes <Dur step="slice-spring-onion"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="boil-soba">
  Bring a large pan of unsalted water to the boil and lower in the <Qty ref="soba"/>,
  loosening them at once with chopsticks. Cook them for <Dur step="boil-soba"/>, until they
  are tender through but still firm to the bite.
</Step>

<Step id="simmer-tsuyu">
  Pour the strained dashi into a small pan with the <Qty ref="soy"/> and the
  <Qty ref="mirin"/>. Bring it to the boil, then simmer for <Dur step="simmer-tsuyu"/> to
  cook off the alcohol in the mirin, and let it cool. This is the tsuyu. Dilute it with the
  <Qty ref="water"/>, one part tsuyu to two parts water, and chill it.
</Step>

<Step id="chill-tsuyu">
  Leave the diluted tsuyu in the fridge until it is cold, which takes
  <Dur step="chill-tsuyu"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="rinse-soba">
  Drain the noodles and rinse them under cold running water, rubbing them between the
  fingers to lose the surface starch. Finish them in iced water for a moment, then drain
  them well, shaking off the water, and pile them on the bamboo tray.
</Step>

<Step id="serve-zaru">
  Set the tray of noodles in the middle of the table with the <Qty ref="nori"/>, the
  <Qty ref="spring-onion"/> and the <Qty ref="wasabi"/> in small dishes beside it. Pour the
  cold tsuyu into two small cups for dipping. Each mouthful of noodles is lifted from the
  tray, dipped and eaten.
</Step>
