The story told most often about the salteña is a tidy one: Juana Manuela
Gorriti, an Argentine writer exiled from the province of Salta after her
father's political defeat, is said to have taken up street food to
survive, fusing a Spanish pastry shell with Andean ingredients into a
juicy empanada that customers began asking for by her adopted nickname,
"la salteña" — "the woman from Salta" <Cite ref="uark-saltena"/>.

The trouble is that the documentary record does not cooperate with this
story, and the story's own biggest promoter is also on record undermining
it. Bolivian traditionalist Antonio Paredes Candia is the writer most
responsible for popularising the Gorriti tale, but historian Beatriz
Rossells has since directly refuted it: Gorriti's own 1890 cookbook
contains no salteña recipe at all, and by Rossells's reading, none of the
three Argentine empanada recipes it does contain include chilli or potato
— the two ingredients that make a salteña a salteña
<Cite ref="correodelsur-saltena"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia-saltena"/>. More
damaging still to the legend, a recipe manuscript compiled in 1776 by Doña
Josepha de Escurrechea in Potosí — the only colonial-era recipe book known
to survive in Bolivia — already describes empanadas built on potato and
ají, well over half a century before Gorriti was even born
<Cite ref="correodelsur-saltena"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia-saltena"/>.

What the legend gets right, even if the invention story is false, is the
name's origin, and even that is not fully settled: Rossells traces
"salteña" to around 1830, plausibly to Doña Corina Pueyrredón, a
Salta-born woman selling broth-filled empanadas in Potosí, rather than to
Gorriti specifically <Cite ref="correodelsur-saltena"/>. Either way, the empanada
itself was almost certainly already a Bolivian dish before any exile from
Salta arrived to lend it a name <Cite ref="wikipedia-saltena"/>
<Cite ref="uark-saltena"/>. A false inventor and a real namesake turn out
to be different questions entirely, and the salteña's popular history has
mostly answered the wrong one.
