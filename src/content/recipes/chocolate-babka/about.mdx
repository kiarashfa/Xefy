Chocolate babka is a loaf of enriched, butter-and-egg dough, rolled around a filling and twisted before it is baked. My Jewish Learning gives the name as "little grandmother", and links the older loaf to grandmothers who twisted scraps of leftover challah dough with seeds and nuts on Shabbat <Cite ref="mjl-babka"/>.

Gil Marks, as Sarah Jampel reports him, dates the Jewish babka to the early nineteenth century, when housewives rolled extra challah dough with jam or cinnamon and baked it beside the bread. The Jewish version used oil rather than butter, so it was firmer and slightly drier than brioche <Cite ref="salon-babka"/>.

Chocolate came later. Salon attributes the chocolate babka to a mid-twentieth-century American Jewish invention <Cite ref="salon-babka"/>, and My Jewish Learning places its arrival in New York, where chocolate was affordable and easy to find <Cite ref="mjl-babka"/>. The modern filled babka is associated with Breads Bakery in New York, which opened in 2013. Uri Scheft says in his cookbook *Breaking Breads* that the Nutella was meant to evoke the chocolate-spread sandwiches of Israeli childhoods. The article does not settle who created the modern version <Cite ref="salon-babka"/>.
