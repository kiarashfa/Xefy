Pita is a yeasted flatbread of wheat. The dough is risen before it is rolled into rounds, and the rounds are baked at a very high heat. The water in the dough turns to steam and lifts the two layers apart, which is what makes the pocket.

The flatbread tradition behind it is far older than the oven that bakes pita. The Natufian hunter-gatherers of what is now Jordan made flatbread from wild cereal grains <Cite ref="sci-news-natufian"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia-pita"/>. Charred remains from Shubayqa 1, a Natufian site in the northeast of the country, are about 14,400 years old, and the study's authors conclude that they were flat, unleavened, bread-like products <Cite ref="sci-news-natufian"/>.

The leavening is the point that separates the two. The Shubayqa flatbreads were unleavened, and pita is not: its dough is raised with yeast before it is shaped, so it has the structure to puff in a hot oven. A flatbread with no rise bakes flat, and the difference between the two is the rise.
