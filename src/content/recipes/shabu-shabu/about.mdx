Shabu-shabu is a hot pot in which thin slices of raw beef are swished through a simmering broth
and eaten at once, dipped in a sauce. Its best-known origin account places it at Suehiro, a
restaurant in Osaka's Eirakuchō district. Food in Japan says that Suehiro's own account is that
its owner developed and named the dish in 1952, and that no single origin account is universally
accepted <Cite ref="fij-shabu"/>. Just One Cookbook dates the invention to the early 1950s at
Suehiro <Cite ref="jocsk-shabu"/>. The dish is eaten across Japan, but it is often associated with
Osaka <Cite ref="fij-shabu"/>.

The name is usually explained as an imitation of the sound of meat being moved through broth.
Both sources give that explanation <Cite ref="fij-shabu"/> <Cite ref="jocsk-shabu"/>. Food in
Japan records a second account, also associated with Suehiro, in which the word comes from the
rhythm of staff rinsing hand towels, and presents it as one account among others
<Cite ref="fij-shabu"/>.

Its predecessors are less clear. Food in Japan says that shuàn yángròu, a quick-cooked mutton hot
pot from Beijing, is often cited as an influence, but that the link is uncertain and that a
resemblance does not prove direct transmission. A Kyoto restaurant, Junidanya, traces its own beef
*mizutaki* to 1949, a separate claim from Suehiro's <Cite ref="fij-shabu"/>.

The dish is eaten with two sauces. Ponzu is a citrus and soy sauce, served with grated daikon and
green onion, and the sesame sauce is a creamy, nutty dressing <Cite ref="jocsk-shabu"/>. Food in
Japan says Suehiro called its sesame sauce *goma-zu* in the early days <Cite ref="fij-shabu"/>.
