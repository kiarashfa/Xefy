Poulet rôti is a whole chicken roasted in the oven. The Bresse chicken, a French bird, holds an appellation d'origine contrôlée <Cite ref="wiki-bresse"/>, <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>. Prospect calls it the only chicken in France with its own appellation <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>.

The rules of rearing are detailed. The birds are white, of the Bresse Gauloise breed, and are kept with at least ten square metres of ground each <Cite ref="wiki-bresse"/>, <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>. For their last weeks they are finished in a crate, a practice Prospect calls crate-fattening <Cite ref="wiki-bresse"/>, <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>.

Wendell Steavenson, writing in Prospect, calls the bird "practically the emblem of the French Republic" <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>.

Prospect describes the bird with white feathers, blue legs and a red comb <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>. The chef Antoine Westermann, quoted in the same article, recalls roast chicken as "what my mother cooked when the family was together" <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>. At the Louhans market, a seller advised roasting the bird slowly at a low temperature for almost two hours <Cite ref="prospect-poulet"/>.
