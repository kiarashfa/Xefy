Ranch dressing began as a way of getting a work crew fed, not as a restaurant
invention. Steve Henson developed the buttermilk-and-mayonnaise dressing,
seasoned with garlic, onion, pepper and dried herbs, while working as a
plumbing contractor for remote crews in Alaska in the 1940s
<Cite ref="fortune-ranch"/>.

In 1954, Henson and his wife Gayle bought a dude ranch above Santa Barbara,
California, and named it Hidden Valley Ranch. Guests who tried the house
dressing liked it enough that the Hensons began selling seasoning packets by
mail order, and demand eventually outgrew what the ranch itself could produce
<Cite ref="sbi-ranch"/>. Production moved off-site, the ranch became a
corporate office rather than a working guest ranch, and in 1972 the Hensons
sold the business to the Clorox Company <Cite ref="fortune-ranch"/>.

The dressing did not become a household default until Hidden Valley introduced
the first shelf-stable, pre-mixed bottled version in 1983, removing the need
for a buyer to add their own buttermilk to a packet at home
<Cite ref="fortune-ranch"/>. It has been the best-selling salad dressing in the
United States since 1992, a fixture the original recipe's Alaskan work-crew
origins give little hint of <Cite ref="sbi-ranch"/>.
