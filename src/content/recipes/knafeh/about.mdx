Knafeh's best-known origin story reaches back to the medieval Islamic world: a
popular legend has Levantine dessert makers preparing it to satisfy caliphs
during Ramadan, set variously in Fatimid Egypt or in Umayyad Damascus for
Mu'awiya I <Cite ref="wiki-kanafeh"/>. The story is old enough to feel like
settled history, but the dish it describes almost certainly is not the one
served today. The tenth-century Baghdad cookbook *Kitab al-Tabikh*, by Ibn
Sayyar al-Warraq, includes recipes for qatayif but has no knafeh in it at all,
and the version that does appear a few centuries later, in a thirteenth-century
Andalusian manuscript, is described as a pancake-like dumpling thinner than
qatayif and cooked on a flat pan — nothing resembling the cheese-and-pastry
dish this page describes <Cite ref="wiki-kanafeh"/>.

The cheese-filled knafeh has a documented history that is shorter still. The
food historian Mary Isin has found no record of it before the nineteenth
century, and the earliest known recipes for it appear in an 1885 Beirut
cookbook, *Ustadh al-Tabbakhin* by Khalil Khattar Sarkis, which gives four
versions of knafeh including cheese-filled ones <Cite ref="wiki-kanafeh"/>.
Nablus's particular claim to the dish is close behind: the scholar Khalil
Totah described knafeh as part of Nabulsi daily life as early as 1923
<Cite ref="wiki-kanafeh"/>, and the city has kept the association ever since,
lending its name to both the dish and to Nabulsi cheese, which is named for
Nablus and desalted before use precisely so it can go under a knafeh crust
<Cite ref="wiki-nabulsi-cheese"/>. Akkawi, the cheese used here, plays the same
role and is treated in Nablus's own kitchens as an accepted stand-in for the
harder-to-find Nabulsi.

Whatever its documentary gaps, knafeh's standing in Palestinian life today is
not in question: it is the dish brought out for weddings, engagements,
graduations and reconciliations, its semolina or noodle topping soaked in a
syrup marinated with rose or orange blossom water over the stretch of warm,
lightly salted cheese <Cite ref="newarab-knafeh"/>.
