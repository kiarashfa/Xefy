Katsudon is a bowl of rice under a tonkatsu cutlet, which is a thick cut of pork loin or
tenderloin, dipped in egg, breaded and deep-fried <Cite ref="jfg-yoshoku"/>. Its name joins
the cutlet, *katsu*, to the rice bowl, *donburi*.

Who first made it is disputed. Waseda Weekly's account of the most-told story gives
Keiniro Nakanishi, an eighteen-year-old at Waseda University's senior high school, as the
creator, at a café near Anahachimangu Shrine in February 1921. The article calls this the
oldest account that can be verified from documents, because it is cited in a university
history published in 1967 <Cite ref="waseda-katsudon"/>. Nakanishi did not publicly claim
the dish until 1981, when a national newspaper carried his statement that he was its
founder <Cite ref="waseda-katsudon"/>.

The rival stories are older or undocumented. The Waseda article records a Sanchoan
restaurant story that supposedly took place in 1918, and a Tsurumakichō story set in 1913,
which it does not document <Cite ref="waseda-katsudon"/>. Neither is confirmed, and this
page gives no date for the dish's first appearance beyond the documented 1921 account.
