<Step id="slice-onion">
  Halve the <Qty ref="onion"/> from root to tip and slice each half into thin half-moons.
  This takes <Dur step="slice-onion"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="cook-rice">
  Rinse the <Qty ref="rice"/> in several changes of cold water until the water runs almost
  clear. Cook it in a heavy pan with a tight lid, and leave it covered after the boil for
  the last part of the <Dur step="cook-rice"/>. Keep it warm.
</Step>

<Step id="prep-pork">
  Season the <Qty ref="pork"/> on both sides with the <Qty ref="salt"/> and the
  <Qty ref="pepper"/>. Trim any sinew from the edges and make a few cuts through the fat
  so that the chops do not curl in the oil. Lay each chop between sheets of cling film and
  beat it lightly to an even thickness. This takes <Dur step="prep-pork"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="heat-oil">
  Heat the <Qty ref="oil"/> in a deep, heavy pan to <Temp c={170}/>, checking it with a
  thermometer. It takes <Dur step="heat-oil"/> to come up to temperature and then holds
  steady on a moderate flame.
</Step>

<Step id="coat-pork">
  Set out three shallow dishes with the <Qty ref="flour"/>, the <Qty ref="egg-coat"/> and the
  <Qty ref="panko"/>. Dust each cutlet in flour and shake off the excess, dip it in the egg,
  then press it into the panko on both sides so the crumb covers it evenly. This takes
  <Dur step="coat-pork"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="fry-cutlets">
  Lower the cutlets into the oil, which should stay at <Temp c={170}/>, and fry them for
  <Dur step="fry-cutlets"/>, turning once, until the crumb is deep gold and the pork is
  cooked through at its centre.
</Step>

<Step id="rest-cutlets">
  Lift the cutlets out and drain them on a wire rack for <Dur step="rest-cutlets"/>. Do not
  stack them, because the crust softens where they touch.
</Step>

<Step id="simmer-onion">
  Pour the strained dashi into a shallow pan with the <Qty ref="soy"/>, the <Qty ref="mirin"/>
  and the <Qty ref="sugar"/>, and bring it to the boil. Add the <Qty ref="onion"/> and
  simmer for <Dur step="simmer-onion"/>, until the onion is soft and the broth has taken on
  its sweetness.
</Step>

<Step id="assemble-bowls">
  Slice each cutlet across into strips and lay them over the onion in the pan. Pour the
  <Qty ref="egg-topping"/> over, cover, and take the pan off the heat as soon as the egg
  has just set on top. Divide the rice between two deep bowls, slide the cutlet, onion and
  egg over the top, and serve at once.
</Step>
