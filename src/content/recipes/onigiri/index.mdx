<Step id="cook-rice">
  Rinse the <Qty ref="rice"/> in several changes of cold water until the water runs almost clear,
  then drain it well. Cook it in a heavy pan with a tight lid, and leave it covered off the heat for
  the last part of <Dur step="cook-rice"/>. While it is still hot, spread it on a wide tray and fold
  the <Qty ref="salt-rice"/> through it so that every grain is lightly seasoned. No vinegar goes in,
  because the rice is plain and not sushi rice.
</Step>

<Step id="salt-fish">
  Rub the <Qty ref="salt-salmon"/> into both sides of the <Qty ref="salmon"/> and leave it on a rack
  for <Dur step="salt-fish"/>, so that the salt firms the flesh. This happens while the rice cooks.
</Step>

<Step id="cut-nori">
  Cut the <Qty ref="nori"/> into strips, each one wide enough to wrap a rice ball around its base
  and long enough to meet on the other side. This takes <Dur step="cut-nori"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="grill-salmon">
  Wipe the salmon dry and grill it until the flesh has turned opaque right through and comes away
  in flakes. This takes <Dur step="grill-salmon"/>. Do not overcook it, because it dries quickly
  once the centre is set.
</Step>

<Step id="flake-salmon">
  Flake the salmon into a bowl with a fork, removing any bones as you go. This takes
  <Dur step="flake-salmon"/>, and the flakes are left to cool while the rice is shaped.
</Step>

<Step id="shape-onigiri">
  Wet your hands in a bowl of lightly salted water, shake them dry, and take a portion of the warm
  rice in your palm. Press a hollow in the centre, put in a spoonful of the flaked salmon, and close
  the rice over it. Shape it into a triangle with light, firm pressure, then wrap a strip of the
  <Qty ref="nori"/> around its base just before it is eaten. This takes <Dur step="shape-onigiri"/>.
</Step>
