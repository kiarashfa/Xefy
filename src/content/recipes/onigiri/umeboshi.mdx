<Step id="cook-rice">
  Rinse the <Qty ref="rice"/> in several changes of cold water until the water runs almost clear,
  then drain it well. Cook it in a heavy pan with a tight lid, and leave it covered off the heat for
  the last part of <Dur step="cook-rice"/>. Spread it on a wide tray while it is still hot, so that
  the steam can escape. No vinegar goes in, because the rice is plain and not sushi rice.
</Step>

<Step id="prep-umeboshi">
  Split the <Qty ref="umeboshi"/> open with your fingers and tear the flesh into small pieces,
  removing any stones that are left. Keep the pieces together on a plate. This takes
  <Dur step="prep-umeboshi"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="cut-nori">
  Cut the <Qty ref="nori"/> into strips, each one wide enough to wrap a rice ball around its base
  and long enough to meet on the other side. This takes <Dur step="cut-nori"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="shape-onigiri">
  Wet your hands in a bowl of water, shake them dry, and take a portion of the warm rice in your
  palm. Press a hollow in the centre, put in a piece of the umeboshi, and close the rice over it.
  Shape it into a triangle with light, firm pressure, then wrap a strip of the <Qty ref="nori"/>
  around its base just before it is eaten. This takes <Dur step="shape-onigiri"/>.
</Step>
