<Step id="season-eggs">
  Beat the <Qty ref="egg"/> lightly in a bowl with the strained dashi, the <Qty ref="sugar"/>, the
  <Qty ref="soy"/>, the <Qty ref="mirin"/> and the <Qty ref="salt"/>, until the sugar and salt have
  dissolved and the mixture is evenly yellow. Avoid beating in air. This takes
  <Dur step="season-eggs"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="heat-pan">
  Set a rectangular tamagoyaki pan over a medium flame. Wipe it with the <Qty ref="oil"/> on folded
  kitchen paper, and leave it until the surface is hot enough that a little of the egg sets as soon as
  it touches the pan. This takes <Dur step="heat-pan"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="roll-omelette">
  Pour in a thin layer of the egg mixture, enough to cover the base of the pan, and cook it until it
  has just set on top. Roll it up from the far side towards you with chopsticks or a spatula. Push the
  roll to the far end of the pan, wipe the empty part with oil, and pour in another thin layer so that
  it runs under the roll. Repeat until the egg is used up, rolling each new layer around the last. This
  takes <Dur step="roll-omelette"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="rest-tamagoyaki">
  Lift the omelette out onto a bamboo mat or a board, and press it gently into a rectangle while it is
  still soft. Leave it to rest for <Dur step="rest-tamagoyaki"/>, so that it firms enough to slice.
</Step>

<Step id="slice-tamagoyaki">
  Cut the omelette into slices across the roll, each about the thickness of a finger, and serve them at
  once with the rolled edges uppermost. This takes <Dur step="slice-tamagoyaki"/>.
</Step>
