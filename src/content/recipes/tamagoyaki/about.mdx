Tamagoyaki is a rolled omelette, cooked in thin layers in a rectangular pan. For a long time Japanese
people rarely ate eggs, because of Buddhist beliefs. Food in Japan links the change to Western sweets
made with eggs, introduced in the sixteenth century <Cite ref="fij-tamagoyaki"/>.

Food in Japan says the dish has a history dating back centuries and was likely influenced by Chinese
culinary techniques. It places the dish's popularity in the Edo period, when it was served in teahouses
for breakfast. In the Meiji period Western-style omelettes were introduced, and tamagoyaki blended them
with Japanese techniques <Cite ref="fij-tamagoyaki"/>.

After the Second World War tamagoyaki became widely available as street food, and it appeared in
bento boxes <Cite ref="fij-tamagoyaki"/>.

The two regional styles are told apart by the egg. The Kansai version, dashimaki tamago, uses a high
proportion of dashi, which makes the omelette silkier and more savoury, and Just One Cookbook says it
must be eaten fresh <Cite ref="jocsk-tamagoyaki"/>. The Kantō version, atsuyaki tamago, uses less dashi
and more sugar, so that it is sweeter and firmer, and it travels well in a bento box
<Cite ref="jocsk-tamagoyaki"/>. Food in Japan describes the Kantō omelette as slightly sweet and thick,
and often browned slowly <Cite ref="fij-tamagoyaki"/>.
