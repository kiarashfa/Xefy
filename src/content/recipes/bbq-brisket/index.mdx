<Step id="season">
  Trim the <Qty ref="brisket"/> so the fat cap is an even layer rather than
  removing it, then coat the whole surface with the <Qty ref="salt"/> and the
  <Qty ref="pepper"/>, pressing it on so it holds.
</Step>

<Step id="smoke-unwrapped">
  Set the brisket fat side up in a smoker running at a steady <Temp c={110}/>
  over post oak or another mild hardwood, and leave it undisturbed for
  <Dur step="smoke-unwrapped"/>, until a dark, firm bark has formed on the
  surface and the internal temperature has climbed into the stall, where it
  will hold roughly steady for a long stretch.
</Step>

<Step id="wrap">
  Lift the brisket onto a large sheet of butcher paper and wrap it tightly,
  sealing the seams underneath, to carry it through the rest of the stall
  without softening the bark.
</Step>

<Step id="smoke-wrapped">
  Return the wrapped brisket to the smoker at the same temperature for
  <Dur step="smoke-wrapped"/>, until a probe pushed into the thickest part of
  the flat meets no resistance at all and the internal temperature reads
  close to <Temp c={95}/>.
</Step>

<Step id="rest">
  Leave the brisket wrapped and rest it in a dry cooler or a switched-off
  oven for <Dur step="rest"/> before opening the paper. This is not optional —
  the meat is far too hot to hold together if it is cut straight away, and it
  finishes tenderising as it cools.
</Step>

<Step id="slice">
  Unwrap the brisket and separate the point from the flat where the seam of
  fat runs between them. Slice each piece on its own, cutting straight across
  its own grain into slices about the width of a pencil, for
  <Dur step="slice"/>.
</Step>
