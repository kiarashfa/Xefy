Chocolate mousse is a dessert of melted chocolate lightened with whipped egg white, and often with cream. Its earliest printed recipes in France are from 1768, 1814 and 1827 <Cite ref="fr-mousse"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-mousse"/>.

In French the dessert was called *mayonnaise au chocolat*, and in English *chocolate mayonnaise*. Food Republic reports that no historical record explains the name, and suggests only that mousse and mayonnaise are both egg-based and creamy <Cite ref="fr-mousse"/>.

A widely repeated story credits the painter Henri Toulouse-Lautrec with the dish and its name in the late nineteenth century. Food Republic presents this as a story, notes that earlier printed recipes exist, and says that the mousse probably predates his period <Cite ref="fr-mousse"/>.

The recipe uses raw egg, in both the whites and the yolks, as the dish is traditionally made.
