Vada as a category has a genuinely old textual trail. K. T. Achaya dated
mentions of it to Sangam-era Tamil literature, broadly placed between 100 BC
and 300 AD, and a specific recipe for something called *vataka* — beans soaked,
ground and deep-fried into balls — appears in the *Manasollasa*
<Cite ref="wiki-vada"/>, an encyclopedic Sanskrit text commissioned for the
court of the Western Chalukya king Someshvara III, who reigned from 1127 to
1138, and now considered the earliest surviving non-medical Indian text
devoted to food. Its version of the recipe is specific enough to describe:
dumplings of urad dal and pepper soaked in yoghurt, closer to today's dahi
vada than to the drier, deep-fried medu vada this page treats
<Cite ref="lhi-manasollasa"/>. That gives the wider family of lentil fritters,
of which medu vada is one member among many across the subcontinent, a long
and reasonably well-attested history — even though the earliest recorded
version was a yoghurt-soaked dish rather than the crisp fried ring eaten dry
today.

The specific dish this page describes — a ring rather than a ball, made from
dehusked urad dal rather than mung, whipped light before frying — is harder to
date to a single point. Its name is straightforward: *medu* means soft in both
Kannada and Tamil, describing the fritter's texture rather than naming a place
or a person, and its many regional names — *uzhunnu vadai* in Tamil,
*uddina vade* in Kannada, *garelu* in Telugu — are essentially the same word
for the same food in different South Indian languages <Cite ref="wiki-medu-vada"/>.
The food writer Vir Sanghvi has traced this particular version with some
confidence to the Karnataka town of Maddur, though the wider vada tradition it
draws on long predates any single town's claim to it <Cite ref="wiki-medu-vada"/>.

The ring shape is not decorative. A flat disc of the same batter fries through
unevenly, dense at the centre and overdone at the edge; punching a hole
through the middle before frying gives it a consistent thickness all the way
round, so the whole vada crisps and cooks at close to the same rate.
