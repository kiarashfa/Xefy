Tonkatsu is a yōshoku dish, a Western-style cooking tradition in Japan, and its best-known
account places it at Rengatei, a restaurant in Ginza, Tokyo. Both accounts give
1899 as the date the restaurant had a pork cutlet on its menu, and they describe a
thick cutlet of pork loin or tenderloin <Cite ref="jfg-yoshoku"/>.

The first version was not deep-fried. Just One Cookbook describes the 1899 dish as pork
slices sautéed in butter and then baked, with steamed vegetables alongside, and says the
technique of deep frying came later <Cite ref="joc-yoshoku"/>. During the Russo-Japanese
War (1904–1905), a labour shortage led the kitchen to coat the meat in a batter like
tempura and fry it, and shredded cabbage replaced the steamed vegetables
<Cite ref="joc-yoshoku"/>.

The name is a borrowing. Japanese Food Guide reads *ton* as one reading of the character
for pig, and *katsu* as a shortening of *katsuretsu*, the Japanese transliteration of
*côtelette*, the French word for a cutlet <Cite ref="jfg-yoshoku"/>.
