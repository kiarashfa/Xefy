notes:
  techniqueRationale:
    - stepId: fry-base
      text: Frying the doubanjiang and dried chilli in oil before any liquid is added is what draws out their red colour and toasts their flavour — added straight into stock, the same ingredients taste flat and raw by comparison.
  commonPitfalls:
    - stepId: cook-at-table
      text: Meat sliced too thick stays tough even after a long dip in the broth. Slicing it paper-thin, ideally while still slightly frozen, is what lets each piece cook through in the seconds it takes to swirl through the pot.

<Step id="fry-base">
  Heat the <Qty ref="oil"/> in a large pot over medium heat and fry the
  <Qty ref="doubanjiang"/>, <Qty ref="dried-chilli"/> and
  <Qty ref="sichuan-pepper"/> for <Dur step="fry-base"/>, until the oil turns
  red and fragrant. Add the <Qty ref="garlic"/>, <Qty ref="ginger"/> and
  <Qty ref="star-anise"/> and stir briefly.
</Step>

<Step id="simmer-broth">
  Pour in the <Qty ref="stock"/> and stir in the <Qty ref="rock-sugar"/>. Bring
  to a simmer and cook gently for <Dur step="simmer-broth"/>, then transfer to
  a tabletop burner.
</Step>

<Step id="prepare-dip">
  Meanwhile, combine the <Qty ref="dip-sesame-oil"/> and <Qty ref="dip-garlic"/>
  in small individual bowls for dipping.
</Step>

<Step id="arrange-plates">
  Arrange the <Qty ref="beef"/>, <Qty ref="pork"/>, <Qty ref="tofu"/>,
  <Qty ref="napa-cabbage"/>, <Qty ref="mushroom"/> and <Qty ref="noodles"/> on
  plates around the pot, ready for cooking at the table.
</Step>

<Step id="cook-at-table">
  Keep the broth at a steady simmer on the tabletop burner. Add the
  ingredients in small batches, cooking each briefly in the broth until done,
  over <Dur step="cook-at-table"/> in total, and dip each piece in the
  sesame-and-garlic sauce before eating.
</Step>
