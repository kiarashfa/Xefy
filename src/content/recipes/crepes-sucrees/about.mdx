The crêpe is the best-known food of Brittany, but the buckwheat it began with is older and wider than the region's reputation for it. The Breton regional body BCD places buckwheat in the region's cooking as a food crop introduced in the fifteenth century, and cites the *Catholicon* of 1499 as an early written record of the crêpe, under the Breton name *crampoezenn* <Cite ref="bcd-creperie"/>.

Buckwheat galettes were eaten beyond Brittany as well. The Konbini article, drawing on a historian's thesis, dates them in Brittany and elsewhere in France to around the sixteenth century, and names Normandy, Limousin and Auvergne among the regions where they are still made <Cite ref="konbini-galette"/>.

The two names are a regional quarrel. The galette is the thicker of the two and the crêpe the thinner, and BCD describes the argument over the terms as a "passionate war" <Cite ref="konbini-galette"/>, <Cite ref="bcd-creperie"/>. The first crêperie in Rennes is thought to have opened in the nineteenth century <Cite ref="bcd-creperie"/>.

The sweet crêpe, made from wheat flour, is the version this recipe follows, and it is the least firmly dated. Wheat and buckwheat were used in different periods and places, and no date is recorded for when the sweet wheat crêpe appeared <Cite ref="konbini-galette"/>.
