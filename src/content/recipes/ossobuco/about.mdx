Ossobuco is a cross-cut slice of veal shank, braised with wine and stock until the
meat falls from the bone. The name means "bone with a hole", a reference to the
marrow at the centre of the cut <Cite ref="wiki-ossobuco"/> <Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>. In the Milanese dialect
it is òss bus, and the marrow was once the natural reward of those who ate the
less noble cuts <Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>.

The most cited written traces place the dish between the nineteenth and twentieth
centuries, and a precise date for its first appearance is hard to fix
<Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>. Milan gave it a municipal denomination, the De.Co.,
by a resolution dated 14 December 2007 <Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>.

The traditional version is made without tomato, and the most traditional school
in Milan prefers it that way <Cite ref="wiki-ossobuco"/> <Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>.
A tomato version is also a traditional variant, and it is especially common south
of the Po <Cite ref="dissapore-ossobuco"/> <Cite ref="wiki-ossobuco"/>.

The gremolata takes its name from the Milanese gremolà, meaning reduced to grains
<Cite ref="milanofree-ossobuco"/>. The usual accompaniment is risotto alla milanese,
the typical Milanese first course <Cite ref="dissapore-ossobuco"/>
<Cite ref="wiki-ossobuco"/>.
