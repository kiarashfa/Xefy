Maki is sushi rolled in a sheet of nori, the dried seaweed, with vinegared rice on the outside
and a filling inside, then cut into rounds. Its history is thinly recorded: Food in Japan
lists boxed, rolled and rod sushi in the mid-Edo period <Cite ref="fij-sushi"/>.

Rolled and pressed sushi belong to the same Edo street-food world that gave nigiri. Nippon.com
describes sushi in Edo as a cheap, quick food sold from stalls and from wooden boxes, with fish
such as kohada firmed in vinegar before it was sold <Cite ref="nippon-sushi"/>. Food in Japan
notes that without refrigeration, raw fish spoiled quickly, so fish was salted, marinated or
cooked before service <Cite ref="fij-sushi"/>. The rice in this recipe is seasoned in the same way
as the rice for nigiri.
