Plov's own past is told mostly through legend, and both sources here are explicit
that none of the usual stories is documented fact. One account credits Alexander
the Great, who is said to have ordered his cooks to devise a filling,
easy-to-prepare dish for his soldiers after he took Marakanda, the city that
became Samarkand <Cite ref="smithsonian-plov"/>. Another names the tenth-century
physician and scholar Ibn Sina, sometimes called the "father of plov," for having
written down a recipe for what he called *palov osh* <Cite ref="smithsonian-plov"/>.
A third points to Tamerlane, the Central Asian conqueror who ruled the region in
the thirteen-hundreds, serving the dish to his armies. None of the three is
treated as more than legend by either source, and the honest position is that the
dish's ancestry is contested rather than resolved <Cite ref="natgeo-plov"/>.

What written record exists points further back and more vaguely: the earliest
documented reference to a dish resembling plov — meat, rice and spices cooked
together — appears in the tenth-century Islamic cookbook *Kitab al-Tabikh*, taken
as a possible ancestor of the modern dish rather than plov itself under another
name <Cite ref="natgeo-plov"/>. The word *plov* is itself borrowed, tracing to the
Persian *polow* for rice cooked with meat or fish and spices, and the same root
produces *pilaf* internationally and *osh* or *ash* across Central Asia and the
Caucasus <Cite ref="natgeo-plov"/>.

Far better documented is how differently the dish is made across Uzbekistan
itself. Around Samarkand it is cooked much as elsewhere but kept in distinct
layers at the point of serving — rice, then carrot, then meat — rather than
folded together; Tashkent mixes everything through instead; and the Fergana
Valley's version is known specifically for being made very spicy
<Cite ref="smithsonian-plov"/>. Plov was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural
Heritage list in 2016 on Uzbekistan's submission, a recognition that a number of
its neighbours, who also claim versions of the dish as their own, did not
uniformly welcome <Cite ref="smithsonian-plov"/>.
