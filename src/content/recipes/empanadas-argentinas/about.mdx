The empanada's earliest documented traces are Galician and medieval, well
before any Spanish ship reached the Americas. A carving on the Pórtico de la
Gloria at the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela, made by the sculptor
Master Mateo around 1188, shows a man reaching for what historians read as
an empanada as part of an allegory of gluttony. It is among the earliest known
depictions of the pastry <Cite ref="laregion-empanada"/>. A generation or two
later, the *Cantigas de Santa María*, compiled under Alfonso X of Castile
around 1282, includes in its fifty-seventh song an anecdote about a thief
who chokes on a stolen chicken empanada until he repents, putting the word
and the dish solidly in circulation by the thirteenth century
<Cite ref="wiki-empanada"/><Cite ref="laregion-empanada"/>. The documentary
record only thickens from there: a monastery document from Oseira, dated
1226, records an empanada used to seal a private commercial transaction, and
later monastic accounts from San Esteban de Ribas de Sil obliged tenants to
deliver eel empanadas to the monks every August
<Cite ref="laregion-empanada"/>.

Spanish colonists brought the empanada to South America, where it split into
distinct regional identities shaped by local ingredients rather than staying
one dish. Argentina alone has a different empanada for nearly every province,
and each region defends its own as the standard. Salta's version (small,
juicy and spiced with ground chilli, its potato-and-beef filling known
locally as *recado* and its particular fold called *simbado*) is closely
associated with the northwest, and many Argentines treat it as the archetype
of the older, spicier provincial style. Mendoza's
version, further south and shaped by a drier, wine-country table, typically
leaves out the potato and keeps the filling hand-diced and tightly reduced
rather than sauced. Neither province has ever conceded the other's version
is the "real" empanada, and the disagreement is itself part of Argentine
food culture: an empanada, correctly made, tells you where its cook is from.
