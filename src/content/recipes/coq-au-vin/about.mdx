Coq au vin is a chicken braised in red wine, traditionally made with the wine of Burgundy. Its origin is not known. Fine Dining Lovers states that the precise origins are unknown, and that people have braised chicken in wine since ancient times, so a single moment of invention is unlikely to be identified <Cite ref="fdl-coq"/>.

The usual explanation for a peasant origin rests on the name. *Coq* is the rooster, whose meat is tougher than a chicken's and needed long cooking, and an older bird fits that account <Cite ref="fdl-coq"/>. It is an inference from the name rather than a record of the dish.

The nearest earlier printed recipe is *poulet au vin blanc*, chicken in white wine, in the 1864 English cookbook *Cookery for English Households*. It is a close relative rather than the same dish <Cite ref="fdl-coq"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-coq"/>.

Much of the dish's reputation outside France comes from *Mastering the Art of French Cooking*, published in 1961 by Simone Beck, Louisette Bertholle and Julia Child, and from Child's television series *The French Chef*. Fine Dining Lovers credits her with much of its popularity in the United States <Cite ref="fdl-coq"/>, <Cite ref="wiki-coq"/>.
