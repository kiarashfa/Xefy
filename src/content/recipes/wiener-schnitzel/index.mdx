  techniqueRationale:
    - stepId: coat
      text: Pressing the crumbs on firmly is exactly what a proper schnitzel avoids — a loose, un-pressed coating is what lets the crust puff away from the meat into the characteristic light, wavy crust, rather than fusing flat against it.
  commonPitfalls:
    - stepId: fry

<Step id="pound-veal">
  Pound the <Qty ref="veal"/> between sheets of baking paper to an even thickness of
  about half a centimetre.
</Step>

<Step id="season">
  Season the cutlets on both sides with the <Qty ref="salt"/> and the
  <Qty ref="pepper"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="coat">
  Dredge each cutlet in the <Qty ref="flour"/>, dip in the <Qty ref="egg"/>, and coat
  in the <Qty ref="breadcrumbs"/> without pressing the crumbs on.
</Step>

<Step id="fry">
  Heat the <Qty ref="ghee"/> in a wide pan until shimmering and fry the schnitzels for
  <Dur step="fry"/> in total, turning once and shaking the pan gently so the fat
  washes up over the crust, until golden on both sides. Drain briefly and serve with
  the <Qty ref="lemon"/>.
</Step>
