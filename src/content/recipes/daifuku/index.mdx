<Step id="soak-azuki">
  Rinse the <Qty ref="azuki"/> and soak them overnight in the <Qty ref="water-beans"/>, enough for
  the beans to swell and soften. This takes <Dur step="soak-azuki"/>, and it is done the day before.
</Step>

<Step id="cook-azuki">
  Drain the beans and return them to a clean pan with fresh water to cover. Bring them to a boil, then
  simmer them gently, topping up the water as it reduces, until the beans crush easily between two
  fingers. This takes <Dur step="cook-azuki"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="mash-anko">
  Drain the beans well and mash them, or press them through a sieve. Return the paste to the pan with
  the <Qty ref="sugar-paste"/> and stir over a low heat until it is thick and glossy and holds a shape
  on the spoon. This takes <Dur step="mash-anko"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="cool-anko">
  Spread the paste on a plate and leave it to cool completely for <Dur step="cool-anko"/>, then divide
  it into balls, one for each daifuku, while it is still firm enough to handle.
</Step>

<Step id="mix-dough">
  Whisk the <Qty ref="flour"/>, the <Qty ref="sugar-dough"/> and the <Qty ref="water-dough"/> in a
  heatproof bowl until smooth and free of lumps. This takes <Dur step="mix-dough"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="steam-dough">
  Set the bowl in a steamer over boiling water, cover, and steam for <Dur step="steam-dough"/>,
  stirring once halfway through, until the dough is translucent and firm.
</Step>

<Step id="knead-dough">
  Turn the hot dough out onto a board dusted with a little of the <Qty ref="starch"/>, and knead it
  for <Dur step="knead-dough"/> until it is smooth and elastic. Divide it into balls of equal weight.
</Step>

<Step id="wrap-daifuku">
  Flatten each ball of dough into a round with the <Qty ref="starch"/> on your fingers. Set a ball of
  the bean paste in the centre, draw the dough up around it, and pinch the seam closed. Turn the
  daifuku seam side down. Each one is finished in turn, and this takes <Dur step="wrap-daifuku"/>.
</Step>
