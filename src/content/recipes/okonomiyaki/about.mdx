Okonomiyaki is a savoury pancake of flour, egg and shredded cabbage, cooked on a griddle
and finished with a sweet brown sauce. The two styles most often named are the Osaka
style, where the ingredients are mixed into the batter and cooked together, and the
Hiroshima style, where the batter is spread thin and the ingredients are built up in
layers. Japanese Food Guide describes the Hiroshima layers as batter, then cabbage, then
pork and noodles, with yakisoba noodles inside the pancake <Cite ref="jfg-okonomiyaki"/>.

The two styles were shaped by the same postwar conditions. Food in Japan says that after
the war the United States supplied wheat flour, which spread flour dishes quickly, and
that cabbage added cheap bulk in hard times. It then says the two regions went their own
ways, with Osaka refining the mixed style and Hiroshima adding noodles for a filling,
layered meal <Cite ref="fij-okonomiyaki"/>. Japanese Food Guide dates the Hiroshima style
to after the atomic bombing of the city in 1945 <Cite ref="jfg-okonomiyaki"/>.

The differences are practical as well as historical. Hiroshima uses more thinly sliced
cabbage and less batter, and its sauce tends to be sweeter. Mayonnaise is traditional in
the Kansai style and is not traditionally used in Hiroshima, though it is spreading there
<Cite ref="jfg-okonomiyaki"/>.

Food in Japan traces the dish's earlier roots to funoyaki, a thin grilled flour sweet
linked to the tea ceremony. That account is given without a source, so it is reported
here only as the article's claim <Cite ref="fij-okonomiyaki"/>.
