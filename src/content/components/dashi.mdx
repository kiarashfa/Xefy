<Step id="steep-kombu">
  Wipe the <Qty ref="kombu"/> over with a dry cloth, leaving the white bloom on the
  surface — that is where much of its flavour sits. Put it into a pan with the
  <Qty ref="dashi-water"/> and leave it to steep for <Dur step="steep-kombu"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="draw-kombu">
  Bring the pan up very slowly over <Dur step="draw-kombu"/>, aiming to hold it around
  <Temp c={60}/>. Lift the kombu out as soon as the first fine bubbles climb the sides
  and well before the water simmers: kelp held at the boil turns the stock slippery and
  faintly bitter.
</Step>

<Step id="steep-katsuobushi">
  Bring the liquid to a boil, take the pan straight off the heat and scatter in the
  <Qty ref="katsuobushi"/>. Leave it for <Dur step="steep-katsuobushi"/>, until the
  flakes have all sunk.
</Step>

<Step id="strain-dashi">
  Pour it through a sieve lined with muslin and let it drip for
  <Dur step="strain-dashi"/>. Do not press or squeeze the flakes — that forces
  bitterness and cloudiness into a stock whose entire point is clarity.
</Step>
